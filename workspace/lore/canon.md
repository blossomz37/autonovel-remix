- The landscape is inspired by coastal West Africa (seed.txt)

- The poorest neighborhoods flood seasonally and are rebuilt seasonally; this has continued for two hundred years (world.md)
- The Adim District is built on the eastern tidal flat, half on platforms, half on reclaimed silt (world.md)
- The Adim District floods every wet season (world.md)

## Magic System Rules

- Time in Varanthe is biological and measurable, not metaphysical (world.md)
- Every person carries a finite quantity of living-time called *natural yield* (Assessors' term), *your count* (borrowers' term), or *your portion* (old women's term) (world.md)
- Natural yield is precise, individual, and legible to trained Assessors (world.md)
- Natural yield cannot be extended by medicine or will (world.md)
- Natural yield can be borrowed against (world.md)
- A Ledger House can advance years from a borrower's future to their present (world.md)
- The debt is recorded in a physical ledger book bound in cloth (world.md)
- The ledger gradually takes on the specific smell of the borrower: sweat, cooking oil, soap, and *base note* (world.md)
- Base note is a biological smell with no analogue that Assessors learn to read like a second language (world.md)
- The ledger is the contract (world.md)
- Destroying the ledger does not cancel the debt; the House holds a master copy (world.md)
- Stealing the ledger accomplishes nothing; the debt is in the borrower's body (world.md)
- Experiential interest: Assessors identify specific future experiences as interest — a wedding, a conversation, a decade of good health (world.md)
- When experiential interest comes due, the experiences are taken: the borrower remembers being present but has no emotional residue or specific memory — only a gap (world.md)
- Longtime debtors describe the absence of taken experiences as "the hollow feeling" / *oghere* (world.md)
- Experiences taken as interest are held in the Ledger House's vault ledgers in secondary ledgers (world.md)
- Experiences taken as interest cannot be returned; there is no mechanism for reinstatement (world.md — stated as presented limitation; whether policy or technical is unresolved)
- Borrowed years feel different: colors arrive slightly oversaturated, sound comes a half-beat late (seed.txt / world.md)
- Long-term borrowers develop a particular look: vivid, slightly unreal, present in the way something preserved is present (seed.txt / world.md)
- Children of borrowers sometimes describe their parents as "too bright" (world.md)
- Assessors are trained to read the borrowed-time texture as a diagnostic tool (world.md)
- You cannot borrow more than your remaining natural yield (world.md)
- An Assessor must certify a borrower's count before any contract is signed (world.md)
- Houses always know exactly how much a borrower has left; the borrower does not (world.md)
- When a borrower dies with outstanding debt, the named heir inherits the full balance including accrued interest (world.md)
- The heir's own natural yield is assessed at the time of inheritance (world.md)
- If inherited debt exceeds the heir's natural yield, the House enters "patient observation" and does not foreclose immediately (world.md)
- The contract specifies no reason for patient observation; senior Ledger-Masters know the reason (world.md)
- Houses cannot lend to a person whose natural yield is zero or negative — with one exception buried in the Orokwe Accords that has not been invoked in two centuries (world.md)
- Houses cannot take experiences that have not yet occurred; interest is always drawn from the future (world.md)
- Houses cannot transfer debt to anyone not named in the original contract or its inheritance clause (world.md)
- Houses cannot assess a person without physical proximity (world.md)
- The Houses' reach is limited to Varanthe and its immediate trade network due to the proximity requirement (world.md)
- Ledgers can be read in full by anyone who knows how, including marginal notations and Assessor shorthand (world.md)
- Experiences held in vault ledgers retain some residue of the person they came from; senior Assessors can read them (world.md)
- Assessors are trained, not born, but training selects for a particular perceptual tendency (world.md)
- Assessors read biological time through a person's face, posture, and smell (world.md)
- Assessors describe their perception as reading *density* — some people seem solid, others thin like paper held to light (world.md)
- Adaeze can read within a six-month margin; she has never been wrong (world.md)
- Assessors cannot easily turn off their reading; they read everyone including people they love and themselves (world.md)
- Children under twelve cannot sign contracts and cannot be named as primary borrowers; they can be named as heirs (world.md)
- Children in debtor families sometimes develop borrowed-time texture without having borrowed anything; Assessors call this *secondary saturation* (world.md)
- The Orokwe Accords include a Jubilee clause: every fifty years, all outstanding debts to be audited and debts of the poorest quarter forgiven (world.md)
- Jubilee requires a two-thirds vote of all active Ledger Houses; the Grand Ledger controls enough votes to prevent it (world.md)
- An Assessor's reading is the legal record; a certified count stands in court (world.md)
- Falsifying an assessment carries the same penalty as forgery (world.md)
- Forging an Assessor's certification carries the death penalty (world.md)
- The sequencing plan requires debt calls to happen in a specific order; disrupting the order changes the outcome in unpredictable ways (world.md)

## Cultural

- Standard Varanthe greeting translates roughly as "how is your count?" (world.md)
- Standard response is "my count is good" or, among close friends, an honest answer (world.md)
- Asking about someone's count directly is intimate (world.md)
- Asking about someone's debt is rude — a social fiction maintained by mutual agreement (world.md)
- Customary mourning period before opening an inherited ledger: forty days (world.md)
- The forty-day custom has no legal standing but is nearly universal (world.md)
- Opening a ledger before forty days is considered disrespectful to the dead (world.md)
- The longer an heir waits to open the ledger, the more interest accrues; the Houses do not remind heirs of this (world.md)
- *Ofe onugbu* (bitter-leaf soup) is traditionally eaten when signing a contract; the bitterness is intentional (world.md)
- Some families refuse to serve bitter-leaf soup at any other occasion due to its debt association (world.md)
- Children in debtor families are not told about inherited debt; this is considered protective (world.md)
- Most people in Varanthe learn about their own family's debt as adults, through inheritance (world.md)
- There is no word in the common language for discovering your childhood was purchased (world.md)
- There are many words for the feeling of discovering you owe something you cannot pay (world.md)
- The dead are buried within three days, before the wet-season heat (world.md)
- Bodies are wrapped in undyed cloth for burial (world.md)
- The ledger of the deceased is placed on their chest for the burial night, then removed before interment (world.md)
- The moment the ledger is removed before interment is when the debt formally transfers (world.md)
- The heir does not have to be present for the debt transfer (world.md)
- The dominant faith holds that the dead pass into a state called *the long count* — an afterlife measured in unlived time and unspent experiences (world.md)
- A person who dies in arrears enters the long count depleted (world.md)
- Paying off debt before death is considered a form of spiritual hygiene (world.md)
- Paying off debt often requires borrowing more; the priests know this and most have accounts (world.md)
- *Oghere*: word used in the Adim district meaning roughly "the place where a thing was"; refers to the hollow feeling from taken experiences (world.md)
- Children in the Adim district grow up knowing their parents have *oghere*; it is ordinary and not discussed (world.md)
- Children in the Adim district play a game called *count-and-run* involving tagging and shouting numbers; understood to be about debt, never said aloud (world.md)
- Food in the Adim District: pepper soup with catfish, roasted plantain from flat baskets, palm wine from clay jugs sealed with banana leaves (world.md)
- Old women sit outside the Adim House every morning, not to borrow, just to sit near it (world.md)
- The Assessor Certification Act is framed as consumer protection; it functions to eliminate independent assessors (world.md)
