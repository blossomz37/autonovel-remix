## Magic System Rules

- The fog does not burn off; it thickens at dusk and thins at noon but never fully lifts. (world.md)
- The fog never rises above the bluff road under normal conditions. (world.md)
- The dead can move freely below the fog line. (world.md)
- Above the fog line, the dead must be invited or anchored to a specific object or place. (world.md)
- Dead who cross the fog line uninvited lose coherence — they describe it as becoming tired, then scattered. (world.md)
- A ghost who wanders above the fog line without an anchor spends several subsequent days more transparent than usual, struggling to hold shape or memory. (world.md)
- Every resident ghost is anchored to a physical object. (world.md)
- The anchor is almost never the murder weapon or wedding ring — it is typically a mundane daily-use object. (world.md)
- Destroying an anchor does not kill the ghost; it makes them untethered (a drifter). (world.md)
- Drifters cannot speak and cannot be spoken to. (world.md)
- Drifters move through walls and people, leaving a cold streak and a smell of low tide. (world.md)
- There are currently four known drifters in the Cove. (world.md)
- Anchor objects degrade over time: wood rots faster than it should; ceramic develops cracks spreading from the center outward. (world.md)
- The dead are hungry for the experience of food — smell, proximity, watching it be made — not for eating it. (world.md)
- A ghost not in proximity to a prepared meal within a week becomes irritable, then agitated, then dangerous (bad judgment, emotional volatility, tendency to break things). (world.md)
- The dead cannot physically move objects weighing more than approximately two pounds. (world.md)
- The dead cannot leave the Cove at all — the fog line is their outer boundary; the sea is the other. (world.md)
- The dead cannot lie about their memories, but they can refuse to share them. (world.md)
- The dead cannot be photographed. (world.md)
- The dead show up on audio recordings as a low-frequency hum that varies by individual. (world.md)
- The dead remember their lives with near-perfect clarity up to approximately five years before their death. (world.md)
- Within the final five years before death, memory becomes unreliable and selective — emotional texture is retained but not facts. (world.md)
- Living people who stay in Heron Cove more than 72 hours begin to perceive the dead. (world.md)
- Perception begins as peripheral movement, voices in the next room, a sense someone just left. (world.md)
- By day five, most living visitors hold full conversations with the dead, assuming they are eccentric locals. (world.md)
- Living people who leave after perception has begun carry a residue — they see things elsewhere, hear voices in crowds. (world.md)
- The fog thickens around distress. (world.md)
- The fog rose above the bluff road for the first time in recorded memory the morning Agnes found Carla's body; it receded by noon. (world.md)
- Carla Venn is the first person to die violently in Heron Cove since 1923. (world.md)
- Agnes can hear the dead clearly at any time, in any weather. (world.md)
- Agnes cannot always tell in a crowded room whether a speaker is living or dead; she is right approximately 90% of the time. (world.md)
- Agnes identifies the dead by: slight temperature drop, voice coming from slightly the wrong direction, and the fact that they never quite look at the door when they enter. (world.md)
- Agnes can, with effort and physical contact with an anchor object, pull a ghost's recent memory into sharper focus. (world.md)
- The memory-pull ability works approximately half the time. (world.md)
- Cost of memory-pull: migraine lasting two days and temporary inability to smell anything except ozone. (world.md)
- Agnes cannot compel the dead to do anything. (world.md)
- Agnes cannot banish the dead. (world.md)
- Agnes cannot protect the living from the dead. (world.md)
- Agnes cannot communicate with drifters. (world.md)
- Agnes cannot leave Heron Cove for more than approximately 36 hours without becoming unable to function; she has never tested the exact limit. (world.md)
- The unclassified kelp Agnes uses in her chowder has been slowing something in her since the 1970s; she does not know what stops when she stops eating it. (characters.md)
- Visitors who eat Agnes's chowder sleep very deeply their first night and report vivid dreams of places they have never been. (world.md)
- The Room 4 area smells of ozone even when Agnes has not used her memory-pull ability. (characters.md)
- Douglas can occasionally communicate with one of the drifters — the one leaving object patterns for Agnes. (characters.md)
- One drifter appears to be trying to communicate with Agnes via arrangements of small objects; this has been happening for three years. (world.md)

## Cultural

- Living residents do not ask the dead how they died (considered rude). (world.md)
- Living residents do not ask the dead if they know they're dead (considered cruel). (world.md)
- Living residents do not suggest the dead should move on (considered fighting words). (world.md)
- Agnes enforces these courtesy rules at the inn. (world.md)
- Agnes keeps a physical guestbook: living guests sign with names and dates; dead guests sign on arrival and on each anniversary of arrival. (world.md)
- The guestbook contains thirty-one years of signatures. (world.md)
- The county does not know the guestbook exists. (world.md)
- The Harbor Market has a system: dead customers stand to the left of the stall, living to the right; vendors address them in alternation. (world.md)
- The hardware store stocks unusual quantities of specific ceramic glazes and archival-quality wood sealant for anchor maintenance. (world.md)
- The grocery carries an outsized selection of strongly scented ingredients: good coffee, fresh bread, aged cheese, smoked fish. (world.md)
- The best home cooks in Heron Cove are people with a lot of dead family. (world.md)
- Children in Heron Cove grow up seeing the dead as a matter of course. (world.md)
- The recognized coming-of-age moment is the first unambiguous conversation with a dead person without running; most children hit this around age nine or ten. (world.md)
- Agnes had her coming-of-age moment at age seven, which is considered early. (world.md)
- The local school does not address the dead in curriculum — deliberate policy, not oversight. (world.md)
- Families who tell children not to speak to the dead tend to produce children who leave Heron Cove as soon as they can; they do not always succeed. (world.md)
- The second week of November is Anniversary Week, corresponding roughly to the Drowning Season anniversary. (world.md)
- During Anniversary Week, the dead become more visible, audible, and insistent. (world.md)
- The living treat Anniversary Week like a difficult holiday: preparation, patience, and a lot of cooking. (world.md)
- Agnes does not sleep during Anniversary Week. (world.md)
- Agnes makes chowder every Friday, bread every morning, and a rotating menu planned around what will smell best to current guests. (world.md)
- Douglas's preferred food smells: coffee and bacon. (world.md)
- Vera's preferred food smell: something with vanilla. (world.md)
- Room 4 has never responded to any food smell. (world.md)
- The herons of Heron Cove are larger than the species should be; they stand in the harbor shallows and do not startle. (world.md)
- Locals do not eat the herons and do not discuss why. (world.md)
- The unclassified kelp in the harbor is edible and has been eaten by Cove families for generations; it tastes of salt and something faintly metallic. (world.md)
- Agnes uses the unclassified kelp in her chowder. (world.md)
