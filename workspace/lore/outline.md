## Working Title: *The Hauntings of Hollow & Vine*

**Premise:** Newly widowed Maren Voss inherits her grandmother's crumbling tea shop in the fog-drenched coastal village of Thistlewick, only to discover the shop is staffed by three opinionated ghosts, haunted by a decades-old unsolved murder, and currently under threat from a property developer who may have just committed a very modern one.

## Chapter 13

**POV:** Maren Voss
**Location:** The Hollow & Vine tea shop, back storeroom / Thistlewick parish records office
**Save the Cat Beat:** *Midpoint False Victory (aftermath)* — Maren has just identified the likely killer but realizes her evidence is circumstantial and the one ghost who witnessed the original 1963 murder, Edith, has gone silent.
**% Mark:** 50%
**Emotional Arc:** Confidence → Creeping dread → Determination
**Try-Fail Cycle:** Maren tries to coax Edith back into visibility by brewing the exact tea blend Edith loved in life (dried violet, cardamom, black Assam); Edith manifests but only as a cold shimmer and a single whispered word — *"Ledger"* — before vanishing again.

**Beats:**
1. Maren catalogs what she knows: developer Callum Fitch had motive for both the 1963 death (inherited land dispute) and the recent death of local historian Petra Solano.
2. She visits the parish records office with ghost-in-tow Oswin (the fussy Edwardian accountant ghost) who can read the faded ink without squinting.
3. Oswin finds a discrepancy in a 1963 property transfer — a second signature, smudged, that doesn't match the recorded witness.
4. Maren photographs the page; the camera dies. She sketches it by hand.
5. Back at the shop, she brews Edith's blend. The shimmer. The word. *"Ledger."*
6. Maren realizes: there is a physical ledger. It is somewhere in the shop.

**Plants:**
- The violet-and-cardamom blend is named on a handwritten recipe card tucked behind a loose brick (seen but unremarked in Ch 3).
- Oswin mentions offhandedly that he "kept two sets of books" in his living days — plants the idea that hidden records are a recurring human habit.

**Payoffs:**
- The recipe card pays off in Ch 18 when Maren finds the ledger hidden in the same cavity as the card.
- Oswin's double-books comment pays off in Ch 20 when Fitch's own financial records reveal a second account.

**Character Movement:** Maren shifts from reactive investigator to active hunter. She stops waiting for the ghosts to lead her and starts leading them.
**The Lie:** *"I just need enough evidence to hand this to the police — I don't have to see it through myself."*
**Word Count Target:** 3,200

## Chapter 14

**POV:** Oswin Pryce (ghost, Edwardian accountant)
**Location:** The Hollow & Vine, overnight — shop floor, ceiling rafters, the locked display cabinet
**Save the Cat Beat:** *B-Story deepens* — Oswin's POV chapter reveals his own connection to the original 1963 murder; he was the smudged witness.
**% Mark:** 53%
**Emotional Arc:** Smug detachment → Reluctant guilt → Quiet resolve
**Try-Fail Cycle:** Oswin attempts to move a physical object (the display cabinet key) to guide Maren before she wakes — he hasn't successfully moved matter in forty years; the key skitters two inches and falls behind the radiator.

**Beats:**
1. Oswin narrates the night in the shop after Maren goes upstairs to sleep — his usual patrol of the premises, checking the till drawer out of habit.
2. Flashback: 1963. Oswin was alive, sixty-two, semi-retired. He witnessed the signing of the fraudulent property transfer and said nothing because the man who pressed him was his own nephew.
3. His nephew was Callum Fitch's grandfather. The corruption is inherited.
4. Oswin has been haunting the shop not because he is trapped but because he is *waiting to make it right.*
5. He tries and fails to move the key. He sits beside the radiator and talks to the key all night, which is both comic and quietly devastating.
6. Dawn: Maren comes downstairs, finds the key behind the radiator while sweeping, thinks nothing of it.

**Plants:**
- The locked display cabinet contains Grandmother Voss's personal correspondence — not yet opened by Maren (pays off Ch 17).
- Oswin's nephew's name, Gerald Pryce, is murmured in the flashback — the name appears on a war memorial plaque outside the shop that Maren has walked past a dozen times (pays off Ch 19).

**Payoffs:**
- Oswin's guilt established here pays off in Ch 22 when he is finally able to give testimony (in his fashion) that breaks the case open.
- The key's location pays off immediately in Ch 15 when Maren opens the cabinet.

**Character Movement:** Oswin moves from comic relief to tragic figure with moral weight. Reader recontextualizes every previous Oswin scene.
**The Lie:** *"Witnessing is not the same as participating. I owe nothing further."*
**Word Count Target:** 2,800

## Chapter 15

**POV:** Maren Voss
**Location:** Hollow & Vine shop floor; the locked display cabinet; Maren's kitchen upstairs
**Save the Cat Beat:** *Fun & Games (late, final gasp)* — The cabinet opens and is full of Grandmother Voss's letters, which are warm, funny, and heartbreaking — a brief tonal breath before the dark turn.
**% Mark:** 57%
**Emotional Arc:** Curiosity → Grief → Steely love (grief transformed into purpose)
**Try-Fail Cycle:** Maren tries to read the letters chronologically to find the relevant ones; they are not dated. She tries to sort by postmark; half have no envelope. She finally reads them all in one sitting, in the wrong order, and understands them anyway.

**Beats:**
1. Maren opens the cabinet. Bundles of letters, rubber-banded, some loose. A pressed sprig of heather. A photograph of Grandmother Voss at thirty, laughing, standing in front of the shop with a woman Maren doesn't recognize.
2. The letters are to and from the unknown woman — a friend named Dottie Fitch. Callum Fitch's grandmother.
3. The women were close friends. The 1963 property fraud ended their friendship abruptly. Grandmother Voss knew something was wrong but never knew what.
4. One letter, unsent, drafted in Grandmother Voss's hand: *"I think your husband has done something terrible and I am afraid of what it means for both of us."*
5. Maren sits with this. The ghost Cecily (the teenage flapper ghost, usually irreverent) appears and sits beside her without speaking — a rare moment of stillness.
6. Maren photographs the unsent letter. This is the emotional evidence, not the legal kind. But it tells her she is right.

**Plants:**
- The photograph of Grandmother Voss and Dottie — Maren notices Dottie is wearing a distinctive brooch (a silver hare). The brooch appears in Ch 21 in a new context.
- Cecily's unusual silence plants that she has her own connection to the 1963 events (revealed Ch 18).

**Payoffs:**
- The unsent letter pays off in Ch 23 when Maren reads it aloud at the village council meeting, shifting public opinion.
- Dottie Fitch's existence pays off in Ch 20 when Maren finds Dottie still alive, age 94, in a care home.

**Character Movement:** Maren's grief for her grandmother transforms into active love — she is solving this *for* her grandmother now, not just for justice in the abstract.
**The Lie:** *"Gran never got answers. I can at least get them for her, even if it's too late."* (The lie is that it's too late — it isn't.)
**Word Count Target:** 3,000

## Chapter 16

**POV:** Maren Voss
**Location:** Thistlewick village — the pub, the post office, the harbor wall
**Save the Cat Beat:** *Pressure mounts / Ticking clock introduced* — Callum Fitch files a compulsory purchase order on the Hollow & Vine. Maren has fourteen days before the council votes.
**% Mark:** 60%
**Emotional Arc:** Purposeful → Blindsided → Furious → Focused
**Try-Fail Cycle:** Maren tries to find a solicitor in the village to contest the order; the only solicitor is Fitch's cousin. She tries the next town; the office is closed for a funeral. She calls a legal helpline and is put on hold for forty minutes before the ghosts cause the phone to malfunction out of sheer impatience.

**Beats:**
1. Maren opens the shop for the first time in a week. Small queue waiting. The village has noticed she's been closed; opinions are mixed and vocal.
2. The compulsory purchase notice is taped to the door. Fitch has moved faster than expected.
3. Village pub scene: Maren asks around carefully. She learns Petra Solano (the murdered historian) had been asking about the 1963 land records three weeks before her death.
4. The harbor wall: Maren calls her sister on the mainland. Her sister says *"just sell, Maren, just come home."* Maren realizes Thistlewick is home now.
5. The solicitor dead-ends. The legal helpline hold. The ghosts' interference — Oswin is mortified; Cecily is delighted.
6. Maren decides: she doesn't need a solicitor yet. She needs the ledger first.

**Plants:**
- The pub landlord mentions that Petra Solano was seen arguing with a *woman* outside the records office, not Fitch — plants a second suspect thread (pays off Ch 19).
- Maren's sister's voice on the phone is warm but distant — plants the theme of chosen vs. blood family (pays off Ch 24).

**Payoffs:**
- The fourteen-day ticking clock drives all action through Ch 23.
- Petra arguing with a woman pays off in Ch 19 with the introduction of Fitch's solicitor-cousin Harriet as a possible accomplice.

**Character Movement:** Maren stops being a visitor in Thistlewick and becomes a resident fighting for her home.
**The Lie:** *"If I had proper legal help, I could solve this the right way."* (The real tools she needs are already in the shop.)

## Chapter 17

**POV:** Cecily Marsh (ghost, 1920s flapper)
**Location:** Hollow & Vine, upstairs flat; the shop's small walled garden at 2 a.m.
**Save the Cat Beat:** *B-Story / Ghost ensemble deepens* — Cecily's POV reveals she is not from the 1920s as everyone assumes; she died in 1963, the same year as the original crime, and has been performing the flapper persona as a kind of costume.
**% Mark:** 63%
**Emotional Arc:** Performative brightness → Stripped-back sorrow → Tentative honesty
**Try-Fail Cycle:** Cecily tries to tell Edith (the third ghost, still silent) what she knows about 1963; Edith won't manifest. Cecily tries leaving a message in the condensation on the kitchen window; it evaporates. She tries humming Edith's favorite song; silence.

**Beats:**
1. Cecily narrates her 2 a.m. wandering while Maren sleeps — she tends the walled garden in her fashion, willing the herbs to grow (they do, slightly, which she considers a win).
2. Revelation: Cecily was nineteen in 1963. She was Dottie Fitch's niece, visiting for the summer. She saw Gerald Pryce (Oswin's nephew) forge the signature.
3. She died that same summer — not murdered, but in an accident that she has always suspected was arranged. She adopted the flapper costume because *"if I had to be dead, I wanted to be glamorous dead, not scared dead."*
4. She has been protecting Edith, who actually witnessed the murder directly, by keeping Edith's silence — she feared what would happen if Edith spoke.
5. In the garden, Cecily finally decides: Maren can be trusted. She will tell her tomorrow.
6. The herbs she has been tending are the exact ingredients for Edith's violet-cardamom blend. She has been preparing this without knowing why.

**Plants:**
- Cecily mentions a *"summer of three deaths"* in 1963 — the murder, her own death, and a third she doesn't name (pays off Ch 19).
- The garden herbs growing slightly — plants that ghost intention has minor physical effects (pays off Ch 22 when Oswin's resolve helps move a critical object).

**Payoffs:**
- Cecily's true identity pays off in Ch 20 when Maren visits Dottie Fitch and Dottie recognizes Cecily's description.
- The costume-as-armor revelation recontextualizes all of Cecily's comic moments.

**Character Movement:** Cecily moves from the ensemble's comic heart to its moral conscience.
**The Lie:** *"Being the funny one means no one asks you the hard questions."*
**Word Count Target:** 2,800

## Chapter 18

**POV:** Maren Voss
**Location:** Hollow & Vine storeroom; the walled garden; upstairs flat kitchen
**Save the Cat Beat:** *Dark Turn approaches — final clue found before everything gets worse*
**% Mark:** 67%
**Emotional Arc:** Methodical → Electrified → Sobered
**Try-Fail Cycle:** Maren searches for the ledger behind every loose brick in the storeroom (there are eleven; she finds a 1987 penny, a dead moth, and a child's marble before finding the right one).

**Beats:**
1. Morning: Cecily appears and, with visible effort, tells Maren the truth about 1963 — her identity, what she saw, the third death she doesn't name.
2. Maren listens without interrupting. This is noted by Cecily as unusual and appreciated.
3. The recipe card cavity: Maren finds it on the eighth brick. The ledger is a small cloth-bound book, water-stained, in Grandmother Voss's handwriting — not an accounting ledger but a *witness ledger*, recording what she observed over thirty years.
4. The ledger names Gerald Pryce. It names the murdered man: a surveyor named Thomas Addy who had been about to expose the land fraud. It names a third person present: a young woman, initial only — *C.*
5. Maren looks at Cecily. Cecily nods.
6. The ledger's final entry, dated three weeks before Grandmother Voss's death: *"Someone has been asking about Thomas Addy again. I hope they are braver than I was."* — Petra Solano had found Grandmother Voss before she died.

**Plants:**
- Thomas Addy's name — Maren will find his descendants in Ch 21.
- The initial *C* in the ledger — Maren initially assumes it means Callum; it means Cecily (confirmed this chapter, but the misdirection is available to readers who skim).

**Payoffs:**
- The recipe card cavity planted Ch 3, paid off here.
- Grandmother Voss's final entry connects her to Petra Solano, confirming the murder motive.

**Character Movement:** Maren now holds the complete picture. The shift is from *finding* to *proving and surviving.*
**The Lie:** *"Gran was a bystander."* (She was an active, careful, frightened witness who documented everything.)
**Word Count Target:** 3,100

**% Mark:** 70%
**Emotional Arc:** Purposeful → Unsettled → Quietly threatened → Defiant
**Try-Fail Cycle:** Maren tries to find the third death Cecily mentioned — she checks death records for summer 1963; the records for June–August 1963 are missing from the parish files. Someone has removed them.

**Beats:**
1. The war memorial: Maren finds Gerald Pryce's name. He died 1971. She photographs it — Oswin, hovering nearby, looks away.
2. Records office: the gap in the 1963 records. The records clerk is apologetic and nervous. Maren notices the clerk's hands shake when she mentions the Fitch name.
3. Fish-and-chip shop: Maren eats alone, thinking. Harriet Fitch slides into the seat opposite — polished, pleasant, dangerous.
4. Harriet's offer: double market value, Maren keeps the recipe archive, relocation assistance. *"Callum just wants to move forward. We all do."*
5. Maren asks, pleasantly, what Harriet knows about Thomas Addy. A flicker. Harriet leaves.
6. Walking home: Maren realizes the third death Cecily wouldn't name was probably Harriet's mother — another person silenced in 1963. The corruption has victims on both sides of the Fitch family.

**Plants:**

**Payoffs:**

**The Lie:** *"Harriet is just doing her job."* (Harriet is protecting herself, not her cousin.)

## Chapter 20

**POV:** Maren Voss
**Location:** Seacliff Care Home, twelve miles from Thistlewick; the drive there and back
**Save the Cat Beat:** *Emotional Truth / Mentor moment* — Maren meets Dottie Fitch, 94, who is sharp, remorseful, and has been waiting sixty years for someone to ask.
**% Mark:** 73%
**Emotional Arc:** Nervous → Disarmed → Moved → Galvanized
**Try-Fail Cycle:** Maren tries to record the conversation on her phone; Dottie waves it away — *"I'll only speak if you listen with your ears, not a machine."* Maren puts the phone away and has to trust her memory.

**Beats:**
1. The drive: Maren talks to Cecily, who is riding along (invisibly, but Maren has learned to sense her). Cecily is agitated. She hasn't seen Dottie since 1963.
2. Dottie is in a sunny room full of plants. She takes one look at Maren and says, *"You have Vera's eyes."* (Vera: Grandmother Voss's first name, never used in the novel until now.)
3. Dottie confirms everything: the fraud, Thomas Addy's murder, Gerald Pryce's role. She names Callum's grandfather, Edmund Fitch, as the man who ordered it.
4. She also says: *"Edmund told Callum. He told him as a deathbed gift, like it was something to be proud of."* Callum has always known.
5. Cecily manifests — just a shimmer, a scent of the violet-cardamom blend. Dottie goes very still and says, *"Hello, my darling."* She can sense her.
6. Driving back: Maren has no recording, but she has a witness. Dottie has agreed to make a formal statement.

**Plants:**
- Grandmother Voss's first name, Vera — will be used on the shop's new sign in Ch 25.
- Dottie's mention of Edmund telling Callum *"as a deathbed gift"* — plants that Callum's evil is chosen, not inherited ignorance.

**Payoffs:**
- Dottie's existence (planted Ch 15) paid off here.
- Cecily's connection to Dottie (planted Ch 17) paid off — the reunion is the chapter's emotional peak.

**Character Movement:** Maren gains a living witness and loses her last excuse to stay small.
**The Lie:** *"A 94-year-old's testimony won't hold up."* (It doesn't need to hold up in court — it needs to hold up in the village.)
**Word Count Target:** 3,200

## Chapter 21

**POV:** Maren Voss
**Location:** Hollow & Vine; Thistlewick library; a brief scene at Thomas Addy's grandson's boat shed
**Save the Cat Beat:** *Gathering forces / Quiet chapter* — The one quiet chapter in the back half. Maren assembles her evidence, her allies, and her nerve. The shop is open. People come in for tea.
**% Mark:** 77%
**Emotional Arc:** Calm industry → Warmth → Quiet grief → Readiness
**Try-Fail Cycle:** Maren tries to find a way to present the ledger as legal evidence; a librarian friend tells her it would be hearsay at best. Maren tries to think of another angle; she can't. She accepts that this will be a community reckoning, not a courtroom one — and that might be enough.

**Beats:**
1. The shop is open. The regulars come. Old Mr. Hafod asks for his usual (Earl Grey, one sugar, a cheese scone) and tells Maren she has *"the same stubbornness as Vera."* It's a compliment.
2. Library: Maren finds a 1963 newspaper archive — Thomas Addy's death was ruled accidental. His grandson, Pete Addy, runs a boat shed. She visits.
3. Pete Addy knew his grandfather died wrong. He has a box of Thomas's surveying notes — professional records that corroborate the land fraud. He gives them to Maren without hesitation.
4. The silver hare brooch: Pete's grandmother wore it. It was Dottie Fitch's — given as an apology gift that was never explained. The brooch connects Dottie's guilt to the Addy family's loss.
5. Back at the shop: Maren lays everything on the big table. Ledger, letters, surveying notes, her sketched copy of the parish record. The three ghosts gather. Even Edith manifests — faint, but present.
6. Edith speaks for the first time in chapters: *"Thomas had a flask of tea with him when he died. He never went anywhere without it."* A small, human detail. Everyone is quiet.

**Plants:**
- Pete Addy's surveying notes — the technical evidence that will make the case undeniable in Ch 23.
- Edith's flask detail — plants that Edith was present at the murder (she was Thomas Addy's friend, revealed Ch 22).

**Payoffs:**
- The silver hare brooch (planted Ch 15) paid off — it connects the photograph to the living world.
- Mr. Hafod calling Gran "Vera" — confirms the name established in Ch 20 and roots it in community memory.

**Character Movement:** Maren becomes a hub — people and information flow toward her. She is no longer alone in this.
**The Lie:** *"I need official channels to make this matter."* (Community truth-telling is its own form of justice.)
**Word Count Target:** 2,600 *(quiet chapter — shorter, warmer, more breath)*

## Chapter 22

**POV:** Maren Voss (with significant Oswin interiority conveyed through dialogue)
**Location:** Hollow & Vine, late evening; the storeroom
**Save the Cat Beat:** *Dark Night of the Soul* — Someone breaks into the shop overnight. The ledger is stolen. Maren is not hurt, but she is shaken, and for one hour she considers giving up.
**% Mark:** 80%
**Emotional Arc:** Violated → Frightened → Despairing → Recalled to herself
**Try-Fail Cycle:** Maren tries to call the police; the constable on duty is sympathetic but says without the ledger there's nothing to act on. She tries to remember if she photographed every page; she photographed six of fourteen. She tries to think of another way — and Oswin speaks.

**Beats:**
1. Maren comes downstairs at 6 a.m. to open up. The storeroom door is ajar. The ledger is gone. Everything else is untouched — targeted, professional.
2. The police call. The constable. The dead end.
3. Maren sits on the storeroom floor. The ghosts gather. Cecily sits beside her (echoing Ch 15 when Cecily sat beside Maren with the letters). Edith hovers near the door.
4. Oswin clears his throat. He says: *"I was the witness to the original signing. I remember every figure. Every name. Every date. I have had sixty years to remember nothing else."* He offers to dictate. All of it.
5. Maren gets a notebook. Oswin dictates for two hours. His memory is perfect — the curse and gift of a haunting accountant.
6. The physical ledger is gone. The testimony of a ghost is not legally admissible. But Maren realizes: she has six photographs, Pete's surveying notes, Dottie's forthcoming statement, and now Oswin's complete account. The theft has made Fitch look guilty without helping him.

**Plants:**
- Oswin's dictated account includes one detail not in the ledger: a bank account number for the fraudulent transfer (pays off Ch 23).
- The break-in was clean — plants that Harriet, not Callum, organized it (Callum would have been messier).

**Payoffs:**
- Oswin's sixty-year guilt (established Ch 14) pays off — his haunting had a purpose and he has now fulfilled it.
- Ghost intention having minor physical effects (planted Ch 17): Oswin's resolve during dictation causes every candle in the shop to light simultaneously. Maren doesn't comment. Neither does he.

**Character Movement:** Oswin completes his arc. Maren's darkest moment becomes her most resourced moment.
**The Lie:** *"Without the ledger, I have nothing."* (She has people. She has ghosts. She has enough.)
**Word Count Target:** 3,100

## Chapter 23

**POV:** Maren Voss
**Location:** Thistlewick Village Hall; the council chamber
**Save the Cat Beat:** *Climax — Confrontation* — The village council votes on the compulsory purchase order. Maren presents her case publicly. Callum Fitch is in the room.
**% Mark:** 85%
**Emotional Arc:** Terrified → Steady → Passionate → Exhausted relief
**Try-Fail Cycle:** Maren's formal presentation is interrupted by Fitch's solicitor (not Harriet — a London firm Fitch has hired) who objects to hearsay evidence. The council chair nearly shuts Maren down. Then Bev, the nervous records clerk, stands up.

**Beats:**
1. The village hall is packed. Thistlewick turns out for a spectacle. Maren is at the front with her folder. Pete Addy is in the third row. Mr. Hafod is in the front row with his tea in a thermos.
2. Maren presents: the six photographs, Pete's surveying notes, the bank account number (which Pete has traced to an account closed in 1971 — the year Gerald Pryce died). She reads Grandmother Voss's unsent letter aloud.
3. The London solicitor objects. The council chair wavers.
4. Bev stands. She has found the missing 1963 death records — they were misfiled, not removed, in a box labeled *"Drainage Survey 1962."* She has brought copies. Three deaths, summer 1963: Thomas Addy, a woman named Margaret Crane (Harriet's mother — the third death), and a young woman named Cecily Marsh.
5. Callum Fitch stands and says the records are irrelevant to a modern property order. He is right, legally. But the room has shifted.
6. The council votes. The compulsory purchase order fails, 5–2. Fitch walks out. Harriet does not follow him — she stays in her seat, looking at the floor.

**Plants:**
- Harriet staying behind — she will speak to Maren privately in Ch 24.
- The council vote is 5–2, not unanimous — plants that two councillors are still in Fitch's pocket (a thread for a potential sequel, left open).

**Payoffs:**
- Bev the nervous records clerk (planted Ch 19) becomes the unexpected hero.
- Cecily Marsh's name in the official death record — Cecily, watching from the back of the hall (invisible), finally has her name spoken aloud officially. Her response is to make every light in the hall brighten briefly.
- The unsent letter (planted Ch 15) read aloud as promised.

**Character Movement:** Maren wins the battle publicly. The war (Fitch's criminal accountability) is not over, but the shop is safe.
**The Lie:** *"The truth only matters if it's legally airtight."* (The truth matters because it changes people.)
**Word Count Target:** 3,400

## Chapter 24

**POV:** Maren Voss
**Location:** Hollow & Vine; the walled garden; a brief scene at the harbor at sunset
**Save the Cat Beat:** *Aftermath / Emotional resolution* — The quiet chapter after the climax. Harriet comes to the shop. Dottie's formal statement reaches the police. And Maren calls her sister.
**% Mark:** 90%
**Emotional Arc:** Wrung out → Tender → Settled → Joyful (small, real joy)
**Try-Fail Cycle:** Maren tries to feel triumphant; she mostly feels tired and sad for everyone involved — the Addy family's sixty-year loss, Dottie's guilt, Oswin's long wait. She tries to articulate this to Cecily. Cecily says: *"Darling, you're allowed to feel two things."*

**Beats:**
1. Morning after the council meeting. The shop opens to a small queue and three people who want to shake Maren's hand.
2. Harriet arrives at closing time. She sits. She orders tea (Maren makes the violet-cardamom blend without thinking). Harriet says: *"My mother's name was Margaret. I've known what happened to her since I was seventeen. I've been trying to decide what to do about it for thirty years."* She hands Maren a USB drive — Callum's financial records, the modern fraud that killed Petra Solano.
3. Maren calls the police. This time, the detective inspector from the mainland answers.
4. The walled garden: Maren sits in the late afternoon. All three ghosts are present — Oswin doing phantom accounts on a phantom ledger, Cecily deadheading phantom flowers, Edith simply sitting in the sun. It's domestic. It's peaceful.
5. The harbor at sunset: Maren calls her sister. *"I'm staying,"* she says. *"Come visit."* Her sister says: *"I'll bring wine."* Short. Sufficient.
6. Walking back to the shop, Maren notices the sign above the door. She has been thinking about changing it.

**Plants:**

- Edith sitting in the sun — the first time Edith has been at peace; plants her resolution in Ch 25.

**Payoffs:**

- The chosen vs. blood family theme (planted Ch 16) pays off: Maren's sister will come, but Thistlewick is family too.
- The violet-cardamom blend made unconsciously — Maren has absorbed the shop's rhythms. She belongs here.

**Character Movement:** Maren is no longer a widow inheriting a problem. She is a woman who has built a life.
**The Lie (resolved):** *"I just need enough evidence to hand this to the police — I don't have to see it through myself."* She saw it through herself. She had to. And she was right to.

## Chapter 25

**POV:** Maren Voss
**Location:** Hollow & Vine, opening morning; the street outside; the walled garden
**Save the Cat Beat:** *Final Image* — mirrors the Opening Image (Ch 1: Maren arriving at a dark, closed, fog-shrouded shop, alone, uncertain) with transformation.
**% Mark:** 96–100%
**Emotional Arc:** Peaceful → Quietly joyful → Rooted
**Try-Fail Cycle:** None. This chapter earns its stillness.

**Beats:**
1. Early morning. Maren comes downstairs before opening. The shop is warm. She makes tea — the violet-cardamom blend and also a pot of Earl Grey for Mr. Hafod, who she knows will be first in line.
2. She goes outside. The fog is present (Thistlewick always has fog) but the light is coming through it. The new sign above the door: *Vera's — Hollow & Vine.* Her grandmother's name, finally spoken on the building itself.
3. The queue forms: Mr. Hafod, Pete Addy, Bev from the records office, two strangers who heard about the shop from somewhere.
4. Inside: Oswin is at his corner table, doing his phantom accounts, looking — not happy exactly, but *settled.* Cecily is behind the counter, pretending to arrange the scones. Edith is in the garden.
5. Maren goes to the garden door and looks out. Edith is standing in the center of the garden in the morning light. As Maren watches, Edith slowly becomes less visible — not frightening, not sudden, but gradual. Like fog burning off. She turns once, looks at Maren, and smiles. Then she is gone.
6. Maren stands in the doorway for a moment. Then she hears the bell above the shop door and Mr. Hafod's voice: *"Earl Grey, one sugar, and I hope those are cheese scones I can smell."*
7. She goes back inside. She makes the tea.
