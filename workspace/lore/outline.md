### Ch 1: *What Agnes Cooks For*
- **POV:** Agnes
- **Location:** Bellweather Inn — kitchen, dining room, front porch
- **Save the Cat beat:** Opening Image
- **% mark:** 0–4%
- **Emotional arc:** Settled, practiced contentment → the first hairline crack of unease
- **Try-fail cycle:** N/A (establishing scene)
- **Beats:**
  1. Agnes at 5 a.m., making bread. The kitchen described from the inside out: the smell of yeast and the particular cold of a north-facing room, the fog pressing against the window glass. She moves through the kitchen the way water moves through a familiar channel — no wasted motion, no thought required.
  2. She sets eight places at the dining table. Seven for the permanent guests; one for Douglas, whose coffee mug she places last, touching the hairline crack that appeared last Tuesday. She notes it in the small ledger she keeps in her apron pocket.
  3. The guests arrive for breakfast. Vera enters first, adjusts the salt cellar without comment. Douglas materializes near the sideboard, already holding his mug. Room 4 is already seated — Agnes never sees him arrive. She cooks; they gather; this is the arrangement. The scene establishes the inn's social physics without explaining them.
  4. Agnes steps onto the front porch with her own coffee. Below the fog line, the harbor is invisible. Above it, the bluff road is clear and cold and empty. She stands sideways to the door, as always, checking. Nothing unusual. She goes back inside.
  5. Final beat: Agnes opens the guestbook to make a routine notation and pauses. The last entry — Harold Fitch, 1989 — has been on the same page for thirty years. She turns the page. The next page is blank. She closes the book.
- **Plants:** The guestbook's blank page. The hairline crack in Douglas's mug. Room 4 already seated (he never arrives). The fog line's behavior. Agnes's sideways-door habit.
- **Payoffs:** None yet.
- **Character movement:** Agnes is established in her lie — the inn as a closed, maintained system. The blank guestbook page is the first suggestion the system is not closed.
- **The lie:** Fully operational. Agnes's competence and routine are the lie made physical. Everything is in its place.
- **~Word count target:** 3,200

### Ch 2: *The Courtesy Rules*
- **POV:** Agnes
- **Location:** Bellweather Inn — east parlor, Agnes's private quarters; brief scene at the harbor market
- **Save the Cat beat:** Theme Stated / Setup
- **% mark:** 4–8%
- **Emotional arc:** Routine competence → mild irritation → something quieter and harder to name
- **Try-fail cycle:** No-but (Agnes tries to have a normal Tuesday; she mostly succeeds, but something is off)
- **Beats:**
  1. Agnes at the harbor market, Tuesday morning. She moves through it with the ease of someone who has done this for sixty years — the vendors know her order, she knows their dead relatives by name and preference. A quiet scene that establishes the Cove's social economy: the vendor addressing living and dead in alternation, Agnes buying smoked fish and the unclassified kelp she uses in her chowder.
  2. Vera corners Agnes in the east parlor to deliver a formal complaint about the salt cellar. The complaint is real but also a pretext — Vera wants to talk, which she will not admit. Agnes listens with the patience of someone who has learned that Vera's complaints are her form of connection. Vera, mid-complaint, says: *"You can't keep everyone comfortable forever, Agnes. Eventually something comes in that won't be managed."* She says it like she's talking about the salt cellar. Agnes files it.
  3. Agnes in her private quarters, evening. She eats alone — she always eats alone, which the reader should notice. She looks at the photograph of Douglas on the wall: alive, paint-stained, squinting at the camera. She looks at the mug on the shelf — the current replacement, number four. She does not look at the door to the east wing.
  4. Douglas finds her. Brief, warm exchange. He asks about her day; she gives him the abbreviated version. He asks, carefully, whether she's thought about the east wing's roof — it needs work. She says she'll get to it. He says, *"You know what I think? I think the roof isn't what you're not getting to."* She changes the subject.
  5. Agnes checks the guestbook before bed. Routine. She runs her finger down the column of dead guests' signatures, counts them. Seven. She closes the book. The fog outside the window thickens, briefly, for no reason.
- **Plants:** Vera's line about something that won't be managed (theme stated). Douglas's reference to "what you're not getting to." Agnes's habit of not looking at the east wing door. The number seven (Agnes counts the signatures; the reader should count them too).
- **Payoffs:** None yet.
- **Character movement:** The lie is named obliquely by Vera. Agnes deflects. Douglas tries to approach the truth and is redirected. Status quo holds, but the reader now understands it's a choice Agnes is making, not a natural state.
- **The lie:** Vera states the theme; Agnes demonstrates the lie by immediately changing the subject when Douglas gets close.
- **~Word count target:** 3,000

### Ch 3: *A Guest Without a Reservation*
- **POV:** Agnes
- **Location:** Bellweather Inn — front door, kitchen, Room 3
- **Save the Cat beat:** Catalyst
- **% mark:** 8–12%
- **Emotional arc:** Mild irritation (the doorbell, which almost never rings) → genuine alarm → careful, managed calm
- **Try-fail cycle:** No-and (Agnes tries to turn the living woman away; she cannot, and the situation is now worse)
- **Beats:**
  1. The doorbell. Agnes hasn't heard it in years — deliveries come to the back. She stands in the kitchen for a moment before answering, wiping her hands on her apron, already composing the polite refusal. She opens the front door.
  2. Carla Venn. Dark-haired, too many pockets, the specific pallor of someone who hasn't slept properly in months. She has a backpack and two notebooks and she is looking at the inn with the expression of someone who has been looking for it for a long time. She asks for a room. Agnes says the inn is closed to guests. Carla says she knows. She says she's been in the Cove for six hours and she's already seeing things in her peripheral vision and she'd rather be somewhere with walls she can trust.
  3. Agnes's internal calculation: the 72-hour rule. Carla has already been in the Cove long enough to start perceiving. Turning her out now means she'll be alone in the Cove at night, perceiving things she has no framework for. Agnes lets her in. This is the catalyst — not Carla's arrival, but Agnes's choice to admit her.
  4. Agnes installs Carla in Room 3, the only room without a permanent occupant. She gives the towel-hook warning. She says dinner is at seven. She does not explain the other guests. Carla, unpacking, asks: *"Does your basement predate the 1743 survey? That's actually the question."* Agnes stops moving. She asks how Carla knows about the survey. Carla says she's been researching the Cove for two years. Agnes says dinner is at seven and leaves.
  5. Agnes in the kitchen, alone. She stands at the counter and touches the edge of it — grounding herself. She opens the guestbook. She picks up the pen. She writes Carla's name and the date. She stares at it. Carla Venn is the seventh living guest in Agnes's thirty-one years of ownership. Agnes does not consciously register this. She closes the book.
- **Plants:** Carla's two notebooks. Her question about the basement. The 72-hour rule (first explicit statement). Agnes's unregistered count of seven. Carla's mention of researching the Cove for two years.
- **Payoffs:** None yet.
- **Character movement:** Agnes's lie (keep everyone comfortable, nothing falls apart) is immediately tested. She admits Carla not out of warmth but out of damage control — which is the lie in action.
- **The lie:** Agnes's decision to admit Carla is framed as management, not hospitality. She is already trying to contain the situation rather than engage with it.
- **~Word count target:** 3,500

### Ch 4: *Seven at Table*
- **POV:** Agnes
- **Location:** Bellweather Inn — dining room, east parlor
- **Save the Cat beat:** Setup (continued) / B Story introduction
- **% mark:** 12–16%
- **Emotional arc:** Controlled anxiety → something approaching dark amusement → genuine worry
- **Try-fail cycle:** Yes-but (the dinner goes reasonably well; Carla doesn't run; but she's asking the wrong questions in the right directions)
- **Beats:**
  1. Agnes prepares dinner with unusual care — she's cooking for a living nose, which she hasn't done in years. She makes chowder. She uses the kelp. She tells herself this is just hospitality. She does not examine whether she's doing it deliberately.
  2. Dinner. Carla at the table with six dead guests she cannot yet fully see. She can feel them — the temperature drops, the voices from the wrong direction — and she is handling it with the focused calm of someone taking notes in her head. Agnes watches her. Vera watches Carla with the expression she reserves for people who use the wrong fork. Douglas watches Agnes watching Carla.
  3. Carla asks Agnes, carefully, about the other guests. Agnes says they're long-term residents. Carla asks if they're always this quiet. Agnes says some of them are. Room 4, at the end of the table, says nothing. He is watching Carla with an attention that Agnes notices and files.
  4. After dinner, Carla in the east parlor. She's opened one of her notebooks. Agnes, bringing tea, sees the page: it's a map of the Cove, hand-drawn, with the fog line marked in red. The 40-foot contour. Carla has been here six hours and she's already mapped the fog line. Agnes asks what she's looking for. Carla says: *"My brother came here in 2016. He never left. Not died — just never left. I need to know what that means."*
  5. Agnes, alone in the kitchen after Carla goes to bed. Douglas materializes. He says, *"Ag, the thing is—"* and stops. Agnes says she knows. He says she doesn't, actually. She says she'll deal with it in the morning. He says, *"Agnes. I need you to sit down."* She straightens a fork instead. He watches her. The mug develops a new hairline crack.
- **Plants:** Carla's map with the fog line marked. Her brother's disappearance in 2016. Room 4's focused attention on Carla. Douglas's interrupted sentence. The chowder's effect on living guests (Carla will sleep deeply tonight).
- **Payoffs:** None yet.
- **Character movement:** Carla is established as a genuine investigator, not a tourist. Agnes's lie is challenged by Douglas's attempt to tell her something; she deflects with a fork.
- **The lie:** Agnes straightens the fork. The physical tell is established here as the lie made visible.

### Ch 5: *What the Fog Does*
- **POV:** Agnes
- **Location:** Bellweather Inn — Agnes's quarters, the front porch, the bluff road
- **Save the Cat beat:** Setup / Debate (Agnes begins to sense the situation is larger than she's framing it)
- **% mark:** 16–20%
- **Emotional arc:** Restless sleeplessness → cold clarity → the first real fear
- **Try-fail cycle:** No-but (Agnes tries to assess the situation and plan; she gets information, but it makes things worse, not better)
- **Beats:**
  1. Agnes at 3 a.m., unable to sleep. She sits in her quarters with Douglas's mug — the current one, number four — and thinks. She is trying to assess Carla Venn the way she would assess any management problem: what does she want, what does she need, how long will she stay, what's the risk. She cannot make the assessment work. Carla is not a management problem. She is a person looking for her brother, and Agnes knows something about brothers who disappear.
  2. Agnes on the front porch, pre-dawn. She looks down at the fog. It is behaving oddly — thicker than usual, moving against the wind. She watches it for a long time. She has learned to read the fog the way a sailor reads the sky, and what she reads now is: *distress.*
  3. Agnes walks the bluff road, which she does sometimes when she can't sleep. She encounters Gareth Croft, also walking, which is not a coincidence — Gareth walks when something is wrong in the Cove. They stand together for a moment, not speaking, looking at the fog. He says: *"You have a living guest."* Not a question. She says yes. He says, *"Historically speaking, that's not something the Cove accommodates well."* She says she's aware. He says: *"Some debts transfer with the deed, Agnes. You knew that when you bought it."* He walks away.
  4. Agnes back at the inn. She stands outside Room 4's door. She does not knock. She stands there for a full minute, listening. Nothing. She goes to bed.
  5. Agnes wakes at dawn to the smell of something wrong — not smoke, not gas. Ozone. She has not used her ability. The smell is coming from the east wing.
- **Plants:** Gareth's knowledge that Agnes has a living guest (he has sources in the Cove). His line about debts transferring with the deed. The ozone smell from the east wing without Agnes using her ability. Agnes's 3 a.m. thought about "brothers who disappear" — a hint toward Room 4's identity.
- **Payoffs:** Gareth's proprietorial attitude toward the Cove (established in Ch 2 through Agnes's thoughts) is now embodied.
- **Character movement:** Agnes is moving from management mode toward something more like dread. The lie is still operational but she's beginning to feel its cost.
- **The lie:** Agnes stands outside Room 4's door and does not knock. The lie as physical inaction.
- **~Word count target:** 3,000

### Ch 6: *The Third Morning*
- **POV:** Agnes
- **Location:** Bellweather Inn — Carla's room (Room 3), the front porch, Agnes's kitchen
- **Save the Cat beat:** Catalyst payoff / Break Into Two
- **% mark:** 20–23%
- **Emotional arc:** Dread → shock → the specific cold of someone who has already begun to manage the unmanageable
- **Try-fail cycle:** No-and (Agnes finds Carla dead; the situation is now catastrophically worse)
- **Beats:**
  1. Agnes at 6 a.m., noting that Carla has not come down for breakfast. This is unusual — Carla has been early both previous mornings. Agnes waits. She makes coffee. She waits longer. She goes upstairs.
  2. Agnes finds Carla. The scene is handled with restraint — Agnes's perception, not a clinical description. What Agnes notices: the window is open (it was not open when she made up the room), Carla's two notebooks are on the desk but the second one — the one for "the other category" — is open to a page that has been torn out, and Carla is dead in a way that is clearly not natural causes. Agnes stands in the doorway for a long moment. She touches the doorframe.
  3. Agnes on the front porch, looking at the fog. It has risen above the bluff road. For the first time in recorded memory, the fog line is gone. Agnes looks at this for a long time. She goes back inside and writes in the guestbook margin, in small handwriting, what she sees. She does not write what she thinks it means.
  4. Agnes calls the county. This is the Break Into Two — not the discovery, but the call. She is choosing to involve the outside world, which means choosing to let the outside world into the inn, which means the closed system is now open. She makes the call with the same flat competence she uses for everything, and when she hangs up she stands in the kitchen and touches the counter for a long time.
  5. Douglas materializes. He does not say anything. He puts his hand near hers — not touching, because he can't move things that weigh more than two pounds, but near. Agnes says: *"I need you to tell me what you've been not telling me."* He says, *"I know. The thing is — I don't know where to start."* She says: *"Start with Room 4."*
- **Plants:** The torn page from Carla's second notebook. The open window. The fog rising above the bluff road for the first time. Agnes's direct question about Room 4 (finally asked).
- **Payoffs:** The fog's behavior around distress (established in Ch 5) pays off dramatically. Agnes's avoidance of Room 4 (Chs 1, 5) is now broken.
- **Character movement:** Agnes crosses the threshold. She has called the county (opened the system) and asked about Room 4 (begun to dig). Both actions violate her lie.
- **The lie:** The lie cracks here. Agnes's call to the county is the first time she has voluntarily invited scrutiny. The question about Room 4 is the first time she has moved toward rather than away from what she's been not-looking-at.
- **~Word count target:** 3,500

### Ch 7: *Deputy Vale Arrives*
- **POV:** Agnes (with Nora Vale as the engine of the scene)
- **Location:** Bellweather Inn — front door, dining room, Carla's room
- **Save the Cat beat:** B Story / Fun and Games (beginning)
- **% mark:** 23–27%
- **Emotional arc:** Controlled, managed calm → the specific discomfort of being investigated by someone competent
- **Try-fail cycle:** Yes-but (Agnes manages Nora's initial questions successfully; but Nora is better at this than Agnes expected)
- **Beats:**
  1. Nora Vale arrives. Agnes assesses her at the door: the uniform, the practical braid, the way she looks at the inn like she's reading it. Agnes gives her the abbreviated version — guest arrived without reservation, found her this morning, no other living guests. Nora asks: *"Walk me through the morning you found her. Not the summary — the actual morning."*
  2. Agnes walks her through it. She is precise and truthful about the facts and silent about everything else. Nora takes notes without looking at her notepad. She asks about the open window. Agnes says she doesn't know how it opened. Nora asks about the torn page. Agnes says she noticed it. Nora asks if Agnes touched anything. Agnes says no. Nora looks at her for a moment and then writes something down.
  3. Nora in Carla's room, processing the scene. Agnes stands in the doorway. Nora finds Carla's first notebook — the facts one — and bags it. She looks for the second notebook. It's there, but the torn page is gone. Nora asks Agnes if she saw the torn page. Agnes says she saw that a page had been torn. Nora asks: *"You just looked at the wall when I asked about your other guests. What's on the other side of that wall?"* Agnes says: *"Room 4."*
  4. Nora asks to speak with the other guests. Agnes says they're not available. Nora says she'll need to speak with everyone in the inn. Agnes says she understands. She does not say that "everyone in the inn" is a more complicated category than Nora knows.
  5. Nora at the dining room table, end of the scene. She's writing up her initial notes. Agnes brings her coffee — institutional reflex. Nora wraps her hands around the mug and says, almost to herself: *"She came here looking for her brother. That's in the notebook."* Agnes goes very still. She says: *"What was his name?"* Nora looks up. She says: *"Venn. Marcus Venn. You know him?"* Agnes says: *"That's a question."* She goes back to the kitchen.

- **Payoffs:** Carla's stated purpose (finding her brother) from Ch 4 is now confirmed and named.
- **Character movement:** Agnes now knows Carla's brother's name. She knows, or suspects, what that name means. Nora is established as a genuine investigative threat — not hostile, but rigorous.
- **The lie:** Agnes's "That's a question" is the lie in speech form — deflection dressed as acknowledgment.

### Ch 8: *What Vera Saw*
- **POV:** Agnes
- **Location:** Bellweather Inn — east parlor, Agnes's kitchen
- **Save the Cat beat:** Fun and Games
- **% mark:** 27–31%
- **Emotional arc:** Focused, investigative determination → the specific discomfort of learning something you'd rather not know
- **Try-fail cycle:** Yes-but (Agnes gets information from Vera; the information is useful and deeply uncomfortable)
- **Beats:**
  1. Agnes finds Vera in the east parlor, in her anchor chair, with the expression she wears when she has been waiting to be asked something. Agnes sits down across from her. This is a quiet chapter — two women in armchairs, one of them dead, both of them choosing their words carefully.
  2. Vera says: *"You are frightened, Agnes. I can tell because you've straightened that fork four times. Sit down. I'll tell you what I saw the night the living woman arrived, and you will not interrupt me."* Agnes sits. She does not straighten anything.
  3. Vera's account: the night Carla arrived, Vera was in the hallway near Room 3 around midnight. She saw the door to Room 4 open — which it never does. She saw Room 4 stand in his doorway for a long time, looking toward Room 3. Then he went back in and closed the door. Vera says: *"I have been in this inn for fifty years. He has never opened that door. Not once."*
  4. Agnes asks Vera if she knows who Room 4 is. Long pause. Vera says: *"I know what room he's in."* Agnes waits. Vera says: *"I died in that room, Agnes. I have never told you because it seemed impolite. I moved myself to a different room and I have maintained, for fifty years, that I have always been in my current room."* Agnes absorbs this. She says: *"Why are you telling me now?"* Vera says: *"Because something has changed and I think you need to know what the room is before you find out who's in it."*
  5. Agnes in the kitchen after. She is making bread — the physical work of it, the kneading, the rhythm. Douglas materializes and watches her. He says: *"You talked to Vera."* She says yes. He says: *"Ag—"* She says: *"Not yet. Let me think."* He waits. The bread is very good that morning.
- **Plants:** Vera's revelation that she died in Room 4. Room 4 opening his door for the first time (in response to Carla's arrival). Agnes now has two pieces of information converging on Room 4.
- **Payoffs:** Vera's line from Ch 2 ("something that won't be managed") begins to pay off. Room 4's unusual attention to Carla from Ch 4 is now explained as active, not passive.
- **Character movement:** Agnes is now actively investigating, using the tools she has — the dead guests as witnesses. Vera's disclosure costs Vera something (fifty years of maintained fiction) and Agnes lets her have the dignity of pretending it didn't.
- **The lie:** Agnes says "not yet" to Douglas. She is still controlling the pace of revelation. But she is moving toward it.
- **~Word count target:** 3,200

### Ch 9: *The Researcher's Notes*
- **POV:** Nora Vale
- **Location:** Bellweather Inn — dining room (Nora's temporary workspace), Carla's room
- **Save the Cat beat:** Fun and Games
- **% mark:** 31–35%
- **Emotional arc:** Methodical professional confidence → the first crack of genuine disorientation
- **Try-fail cycle:** No-and (Nora tries to build a conventional timeline; the evidence keeps failing to cohere)
- **Beats:**
  1. Nora working through Carla's first notebook. It's meticulous — two years of research on Heron Cove, cross-referenced with county records, historical society documents, and what Nora recognizes as FOIA requests. Carla was thorough. The notebook establishes Carla as a serious researcher, which means her death is not random. Nora makes notes.
  2. Nora interviews Agnes about Carla's behavior during her two days at the inn. Agnes is precise, truthful, and unhelpful in ways Nora can't quite identify. She answers every question and volunteers nothing. Nora asks about the other guests again. Agnes says they're long-term residents and not available. Nora says: *"I understand the town has its own ways of handling things. I'm not here to change that. I'm here because a woman is dead and someone killed her, and that person is still somewhere."* Agnes says: *"I know."* Something in the way she says it makes Nora look up.
  3. Nora, alone in the dining room that evening, realizes she's been having a conversation with a man she assumed was a local — a stocky man in work clothes who asked her thoughtful questions about her investigation and offered, unprompted, that the fog had been unusual the morning the living woman was found. She looks at her notes. She has written down everything he said. She looks up. The chair he was sitting in is empty. It has been empty, she now realizes, for some time.
  4. Nora goes back through her notes. The man said his name was Douglas. She writes this down. She does not yet understand what she's written.
  5. Nora calls the Kettle Falls office. She asks for a background check on Marcus Venn — Carla's brother, last known location Heron Cove, 2016. She asks for anything on Carla Venn's research history. She asks, carefully, whether anyone at the office has worked a Heron Cove call before. Silence on the line. Then: *"Deputy Vale, everyone who volunteers for Heron Cove calls comes back with stories."* Nora says: *"What kind of stories?"* Another silence. *"The kind where you're not sure if you're the one who's confused."*
- **Plants:** Nora's conversation with Douglas (she doesn't know he's dead). The county's awareness that Heron Cove is unusual. Marcus Venn's background check request. Nora writing down Douglas's name.
- **Payoffs:** The 72-hour rule (Ch 3) pays off — Nora is beginning to perceive. Douglas's role as an inadvertent informant is established.
- **Character movement:** Nora's competence is beginning to fail her in the specific way the Cove fails investigators — not because she's bad at her job, but because her tools are wrong. This is her wound beginning to show.
- **The lie:** Nora's lie (procedure will yield the answer) is intact but starting to fray.
- **~Word count target:** 3,200

### Ch 10: *Below the Fog Line*
- **POV:** Agnes
- **Location:** The harbor market, the cannery district, Bertrand Luce's cannery
- **Save the Cat beat:** Fun and Games
- **% mark:** 35–39%
- **Emotional arc:** Purposeful, investigative focus → the specific unease of learning that the problem is older than she thought
- **Try-fail cycle:** No-but (Agnes goes to Bertrand Luce for information about 1923; she gets it, but it raises a worse question)
- **Beats:**
  1. Agnes below the fog line for the first time since Carla's death. The fog is still behaving oddly — thicker, slower, moving wrong. She notes this. She walks through the cannery district, which she rarely visits, and feels the difference between above and below the line: the softened time, the way sounds arrive slightly late, the dead moving freely through the streets.
  2. Agnes at the cannery. Bertrand Luce is inside, as always, sitting in the particular chair he has occupied for forty-six years. Agnes brings good coffee and sits with him. She asks about 1923. He says: *"The survey man. Yes. He came back every summer."* Pause. *"Some things you see once and can't stop seeing."*
  3. Agnes asks what Olmstead saw. Bertrand is quiet for a long time. He says: *"There was a woman. 1923. She came looking for someone, same as your living woman. She found what she was looking for."* Agnes asks what happened to her. Bertrand says: *"She didn't leave."* Agnes asks if she's still in the Cove. Bertrand says: *"Not the way you mean."* Agnes asks if she's a drifter. Bertrand says nothing, which is yes.
  4. Agnes asks about the cannery floor — what's under it. Bertrand says: *"Things that need a roof."* Agnes asks if it's the Croft arrangement. Long pause. Bertrand says: *"The Crofts think they made a contract. What they made was a record. The contract was already there."* Agnes asks who made the original contract. Bertrand says: *"The Quiet Town dead. Before the 1743 settlement. They agreed to stay below the fog line. In exchange for—"* He stops. Agnes waits. He says: *"In exchange for the inn staying open."*
  5. Agnes walks back up to the bluff road. She stands at the fog line and looks at the inn. The Bellweather Inn. The inn staying open. She has been running the inn for sixty years and she has never understood that she was not the innkeeper. She was the terms of the contract.
- **Plants:** The 1923 woman who came looking for someone and became a drifter (parallel to Carla). The Quiet Town contract requiring the inn to stay open. Bertrand's knowledge of what's under the cannery floor.
- **Payoffs:** Bertrand's character (established in the World Bible) is now plot-functional. The Croft arrangement's true nature begins to emerge.
- **Character movement:** Agnes's understanding of her own situation shifts fundamentally. She is not running a B&B. She is fulfilling a contract she didn't know she'd signed.
- **The lie:** The lie is challenged at the structural level — Agnes has been "keeping everyone comfortable" not just as a personal coping mechanism but as a cosmic obligation she didn't understand.
- **~Word count target:** 3,500

### Ch 11: *Gareth Makes His Move*
- **POV:** Agnes
- **Location:** Bellweather Inn — front parlor, Agnes's kitchen
- **Save the Cat beat:** Fun and Games / Bad Guys Close In (early)
- **% mark:** 39–43%
- **Emotional arc:** Unsettled, still processing Ch 10's revelation → cold anger → the particular exhaustion of a cold war heating up

He stands in the doorway of the front parlor — his habit, the position of being able to leave — and says he's come to offer his assistance with the county investigation.
