### Ch 1: The Texture of a Tuesday
- **POV:** Adaeze, third-person limited
- **Location:** House of Adim, Adim District
- **Save the Cat beat:** Opening Image
- **% mark:** 0–4%
- **Emotional arc:** Professional competence → first hairline crack
- **Try-fail cycle:** Yes-and (establishing scene — the system works, for now)
- **Beats:**
  1. Adaeze conducts a routine assessment: a forty-year-old fisherman wants to borrow three years against his remaining count of nineteen. She reads him in under a minute. Nineteen years, maybe twenty if the rains are kind. She stamps the form. He thanks her with the particular relief of someone who has been told they are not yet running out.
  2. The sensory world of the Adim district established through Adaeze's commute home: the knock of boats against pilings, children playing count-and-run, the smell of palm kernel burning somewhere east. She moves through it with the ease of someone who has lived here her whole life and stopped seeing it.
  3. She passes the old women who sit outside the House every morning. One of them calls out the standard greeting — *how is your count?* Adaeze answers: *my count is good.* She has been saying this for two years. She touches the left side of her ribcage without noticing.
  4. At home, she eats standing up, going through case files. On the shelf above her desk: the ledger. Still sealed. She has been stepping around it for eight months the way you step around a piece of furniture you've decided to move but haven't yet.
  5. She goes to bed. In the dark she reads herself — the Assessor's habit, involuntary — and gets the same result she has been getting for two years. Thin. She tells herself it is stress.
- **Plants:** The ledger on the shelf (object). The ribcage-touching gesture (action). The phrase "my count is good" as a lie she tells herself (dialogue). The fisherman's relief as a mirror for what Adaeze cannot feel about her own situation (symbolic).
- **Payoffs:** None yet.
- **Character movement:** We understand Adaeze's competence, her professional armor, and the thing she is not looking at. The ledger is present before it is opened.
- **The lie:** Fully operational. She is a fair functionary of a necessary system. She stamps the forms. She goes home.
- **~Word count target:** 3,000

### Ch 2: What the Borrowers Know
- **POV:** Adaeze, third-person limited
- **Location:** House of Adim; Adim District market
- **Save the Cat beat:** Setup / Theme Stated
- **% mark:** 4–8%
- **Emotional arc:** Routine professional day → unsettled by something she cannot name
- **Try-fail cycle:** Yes-but (she does her job well, but the job is starting to feel different)
- **Beats:**
  1. Adaeze trains Amara on a live assessment. The subject is a woman in her mid-thirties, third-generation debtor, wanting to borrow two years to keep her weaving business through a bad season. Amara reads her as forty — confused, apologetic. Adaeze reads her as thirty-six, count of eleven years. She approves the loan. She does not tell the woman her count. This is standard practice. Amara asks why not afterward.
  2. Adaeze explains the policy: borrowers are entitled to know their certified count, but the House does not volunteer it unless asked. Amara asks if that's the same as withholding it. Adaeze says it is not. *The Accords provide for disclosure on request.* Amara writes this down. The scene is the Theme Stated beat — spoken by Amara, not Adaeze: *"But she didn't know to ask."*
  3. Obi Adim-Eze calls Adaeze into his office. He asks, carefully, whether she has opened the ledger yet. She says no. He says the sixty-day review period is still available to her. He straightens the objects on his desk. He offers tea. She declines. She asks if there is something she should know before she opens it. He says: *"In my experience, the ledgers that are hardest to read are the ones where the borrower loved someone very much."*
  4. Adaeze walks home through the market. She buys bitter-leaf from a stall — she doesn't know why, she never cooks it. The vendor wraps it in banana leaf. The smell is specific and wrong in a way she cannot articulate.
  5. She stands in her kitchen holding the bitter-leaf and does not cook anything.
- **Plants:** Amara's question — *"But she didn't know to ask"* — as the novel's thematic spine (dialogue). Obi's non-answer (action). The bitter-leaf (object — will pay off when Adaeze reads the birthday contract). The policy of non-disclosure as a system detail that will become personal.
- **Payoffs:** None yet.
- **Character movement:** Amara is introduced as mirror and conscience. Obi's evasion plants the sense that he knows something. Adaeze's purchase of bitter-leaf is the first unconscious movement toward the ledger.
- **The lie:** Challenged mildly by Amara's question, which Adaeze deflects with policy language.
- **~Word count target:** 3,200

### Ch 3: The Smell of Base Note
- **POV:** Adaeze, third-person limited
- **Location:** Adaeze's home
- **Save the Cat beat:** Catalyst
- **% mark:** 8–13%
- **Emotional arc:** Avoidance → the thing she has been avoiding arrives
- **Try-fail cycle:** No-but (she opens the ledger; what she finds is worse than she feared, but she doesn't yet understand how much worse)
- **Beats:**
  1. Morning. Adaeze wakes before light, which she has been doing for weeks. She lies still and reads herself and gets thin. She gets up. She makes tea. She stands in front of the shelf.
  2. She opens the ledger. The smell hits first — shea butter, woodsmoke, and underneath, the base note. Her mother's count, biological and specific, a smell she has never encountered in her professional life because she has never assessed her mother. She sits down on the floor. This is not a decision.
  3. She reads the first contract. Dated twenty-seven years ago. Her mother, twenty-three, dying of chest fever. Natural yield at signing: thirty-one years. Collateral: the natural yield of named heir Adaeze Mwenye, currently aged four, assessed yield pending maturation. Amount borrowed: eleven years. She reads it three times. Her mother had been dying. She had been four. She had not known.
  4. She reads the second contract. Dated twenty-three years ago. Her mother, twenty-seven. Another five years borrowed. Purpose: *business expansion, cloth trade, primary income source for household.* Adaeze had been eight. She had been in school. She had had new sandals that year.
  5. She stops reading. She closes the ledger. She sits with it in her lap and smells her mother and does not move for a long time. She is not crying. She is doing what Assessors do when they read something they need to understand: she is being very still.
- **Plants:** The seven-contract structure (she has only read two — the remaining five will be discovered across the next several chapters). The phrase *assessed yield pending maturation* as the mechanism that will become her crisis. The smell of the ledger as a recurring sensory anchor.
- **Payoffs:** The bitter-leaf from Ch 2 — the birthday soup is now legible as something other than tradition (partial payoff; full payoff comes in Ch 5).
- **Character movement:** The catalyst lands. Adaeze now knows the first layer. She does not yet know the full debt, the sequencing, or that she is already in default. She knows her mother was dying and chose her.
- **The lie:** First direct challenge. She has been a fair functionary of a necessary system. She was four years old when the system first processed her.
- **~Word count target:** 3,500

### Ch 4: Compound
- **POV:** Adaeze, third-person limited
- **Location:** House of Adim; Grand Ledger Quarter (exterior)
- **Save the Cat beat:** Debate
- **% mark:** 13–18%
- **Emotional arc:** Shock → the specific cold of someone doing math they don't want to finish
- **Try-fail cycle:** No-and (she tries to understand the full scope of the debt; the numbers are worse than she calculated, and she discovers she is already in default)
- **Beats:**
  1. Adaeze goes to work. She does her assessments. She stamps her forms. She is very good at this. Amara notices something is wrong and does not ask. This is the correct instinct and Adaeze is grateful for it.
  2. During her lunch break she reads contracts three through five in the ledger. Each one extends her mother's life. Each one deepens the collateral against Adaeze's count. By the fifth contract, her mother is forty-three and the total borrowed time against Adaeze's yield is fifty-one years. Adaeze is thirty-one. She does the arithmetic. She does it again.
  3. She reads the inheritance clause. Standard language, four hundred years old, the Orokwe Accords verbatim: *inheritance of temporal obligation shall pass without restriction to named heirs upon the death of the primary holder.* Her name has been in this contract since she was four years old. She did not sign it. She was not asked.
  4. She walks to the Grand Ledger Quarter after work. She stands outside the building and looks at it. She has been inside twice, for professional consultations. She knows what the basement smells like. She stands outside for twenty minutes and then goes home.
  5. At home she reads contracts six and seven. Contract seven was signed eleven years ago, when the chest fever returned. Her mother was forty-three. The amount borrowed: twenty-two more years. Total debt against Adaeze's yield: seventy-three years. Adaeze is thirty-one. She has been in default since her twenty-ninth birthday. In the margin of contract seven, in her mother's handwriting: *She will be angry. She should be angry. Tell her I knew.*
- **Plants:** The marginal note (object/dialogue — will become the novel's emotional crux in Act III). The Grand Ledger building as a physical destination she approaches but does not enter (action — she will enter it in Ch 10). The default status as a legal fact that will drive the plot.
- **Payoffs:** The full debt structure is now visible. The *assessed yield pending maturation* clause from Ch 3 now resolves: Adaeze's count was always the collateral.
- **Character movement:** Adaeze now knows the full scope. She also knows her mother knew, at the end. The grief must now contain both the love and the theft simultaneously. She cannot separate them.
- **The lie:** Severely damaged. She cannot be a fair functionary of a system that processed her at age four.
- **~Word count target:** 3,500

### Ch 5: What the Soup Was For
- **POV:** Adaeze, third-person limited
- **Location:** Adaeze's home; memory of her mother's kitchen
- **Save the Cat beat:** Debate (continued) — the quiet chapter
- **% mark:** 18–21%
- **Emotional arc:** Cold arithmetic → grief that has nowhere clean to go
- **Try-fail cycle:** No-but (she tries to find a version of her mother that is simply a villain, or simply a saint; she cannot find either)
- **Beats:**
  1. Adaeze does not go to work. She calls in with a chest complaint — the irony of this lands on her in the morning and she sits with it for a while. She makes tea. She does not make bitter-leaf soup.
  2. Memory chapter, rendered in close free indirect discourse: Adaeze at eight, at twelve, at seventeen, watching her mother cook. The specific texture of a childhood that was present and well-fed and stable. Her mother humming. New sandals. School fees paid. A mother who was alive past the fever that should have taken her when Adaeze was four. All of it purchased. All of it real.
  3. She reads the Assessor's notations in the ledger margins — the professional shorthand she knows how to read. Her mother's base note, tracked year by year. The texture of borrowed time developing slowly: colors too saturated, sounds arriving late. Her mother had the look. Adaeze had noticed it and attributed it to age.
  4. She finds the birthday entry. Contract two, signed the year Adaeze turned eight, includes a clause she missed on first reading: *experiential interest, first collection, to be assessed at time of borrower's choosing.* Her mother chose. She chose to pay the first interest installment on Adaeze's eighth birthday. The soup was the ritual of a woman who understood, every year, what she had spent.
  5. Adaeze makes the soup. She eats it standing up. It is bitter. She finishes it.
- **Plants:** The memory of her mother's look — *too bright* — as the borrowed-time texture (symbolic, pays off in Ch 9 when Adaeze sees it in herself). The experiential interest clause as a mechanism that will become central to the plot's systemic layer.
- **Payoffs:** The bitter-leaf soup from Ch 2 fully resolves. The *too bright* look, mentioned in Ch 1 as an Assessor's diagnostic, is now personal.
- **Character movement:** Adaeze cannot resolve her mother into a simple category. This is the novel's central emotional fact, and she now knows it. She has stopped avoiding the ledger. She has started reading it as an Assessor reads — looking for the mechanism, not just the feeling.
- **The lie:** The lie about being a fair functionary is now secondary to a deeper question: what does she owe her mother? She does not know yet that these questions are the same question.
- **~Word count target:** 3,000

### Ch 6: Break Into Two — She Files the Review
- **POV:** Adaeze, third-person limited
- **Location:** House of Adim; Adim District streets
- **Save the Cat beat:** Break Into Two
- **% mark:** 21–24%
- **Emotional arc:** Grief → decision (active, chosen, costly)
- **Try-fail cycle:** Yes-but (she formally initiates the debt review — the system acknowledges her right to do this, but Kofi Asante appears, and something in his manner is wrong)
- **Beats:**
  1. Adaeze returns to work. She tells Obi she is invoking the sixty-day inherited assessment review under the Accords. He straightens everything on his desk. He says: *"The Accords provide for this. I'll file the paperwork."* He asks if she wants tea. She says yes this time, because she needs him to sit down and look at her. She says: *"I need to know if you knew."* He says he did not know the specifics of her mother's contracts. She believes him. She does not believe he has told her everything he knows.
  2. She formally requests the House of Adim's copy of the Mwenye ledger master record — the institutional copy the House holds, separate from the personal ledger. This is her right as the heir under review. Obi processes the request. The master record arrives within the hour. It matches the personal ledger exactly, except for one additional notation she does not yet understand: a small symbol in the margin of contract seven, in a hand that is not her mother's and not the House of Adim's standard notation. She photographs it.
  3. Kofi Asante appears at the House of Adim that afternoon — ostensibly a routine inter-House consultation, the kind that happens twice a year. He and Adaeze have lunch. He is warm, funny, slightly too interested in how she is doing. He says, *"Honestly, the inherited debt cases are the worst."* He asks if she has filed a review. She says yes. Something moves behind his eyes that he covers quickly. She reads him — the Assessor's habit — and gets nine years, and something else, something she cannot name, a quality of attention that feels like monitoring.
  4. She walks home. She is thinking about the symbol in the margin. She is thinking about Kofi's eyes. She is thinking about what it means that the Grand Ledger sent someone to the House of Adim the same day she filed her review.
  5. She opens the ledger again. This time she is not reading it as a daughter. She is reading it as an Assessor. The difference is audible in the prose — the register shifts, becomes more clinical, more precise. She is looking for the mechanism.
- **Plants:** The unidentified margin symbol (object — pays off in Ch 10 when she identifies it as a sequencing notation). Kofi's monitoring role (action — he will be revealed as the Grand Ledger's instrument in Ch 11). The shift from daughter-reading to Assessor-reading as a character movement that will define Act II.
- **Payoffs:** Obi's evasion from Ch 2 partially resolves — he did not know the specifics, but he knows something. His non-answer is now legible as a choice.
- **Character movement:** This is the Break Into Two. Adaeze chooses to investigate. She is no longer reacting to the ledger; she is using her professional skills on it. She has crossed the threshold from grief into inquiry.
- **The lie:** She is beginning to use the lie differently — *I am a fair functionary of a necessary system* is becoming *I know how this system works, and I am going to use that knowledge.* The lie is transforming into a tool. This is more dangerous than the original form.
- **~Word count target:** 3,500

### Ch 7: The Debtors' Assembly
- **POV:** Adaeze, third-person limited
- **Location:** Adim District; Debtors' Assembly meeting space (a room above a fish market)
- **Save the Cat beat:** B Story / Fun and Games begin
- **% mark:** 24–28%
- **Emotional arc:** Controlled investigation mode → unsettled by someone who has already done the math she is just starting
- **Try-fail cycle:** Yes-but (she gets information from Sena; the information is useful and also implicates her in a way she didn't expect)
- **Beats:**
  1. Amara tells Adaeze, quietly, that she has been to a Debtors' Assembly meeting. She says this the way someone confesses something they expect to be reprimanded for. Adaeze does not reprimand her. She asks what they talked about. Amara says: *"The Jubilee clause. And a woman named Sena Tawiah who knows the Accords better than anyone I've ever heard."*
  2. Adaeze attends an Assembly meeting. The room above the fish market is full — thirty, forty people, most of them from the tidal flat neighborhoods, most of them with the particular look of long-term debtors. Sena speaks. She is short and still and the room listens. She quotes the Jubilee clause from memory. She explains compound interest in plain language. She says: *"The Houses have been saying 'not yet' for four hundred years. The Accords do not say 'when the Houses are ready.' They say every fifty years. We are one hundred and sixty years past the last audit."*
  3. After the meeting, Sena approaches Adaeze directly. She knows who she is. *"Adaeze Mwenye. You've been stamping forms for ten years. I'm asking you to read one."* She hands Adaeze a document — not a debt ledger, a pattern analysis. Debt calls across the Adim district over the past twenty years, mapped against natural deaths. The timing is not random. Sena does not know why. She wants an Assessor's eyes on it.
  4. Adaeze takes the document. She does not commit to anything. She says she will look at it. Sena says: *"I'm not asking you to trust me. I'm asking you to do the math."*
  5. Walking home, Adaeze reads the pattern analysis. It is rough — Sena is not an Assessor, she does not have access to the full ledger records — but the shape of it is visible. The debt calls are clustering. They are not random. They are sequential.
- **Plants:** The pattern analysis document (object — Adaeze will spend the next several chapters building a more complete version). Sena's settlement offer letter, mentioned obliquely — she is distracted during the meeting, checks her bag twice (action — the letter will be revealed in Ch 15). The Jubilee clause as a potential mechanism (structural — will become the Act III plan).
- **Payoffs:** Amara's question from Ch 2 (*"But she didn't know to ask"*) resonates here — Sena has been asking for sixty years and has been ignored.
- **Character movement:** The B Story is established: Sena and the Assembly represent the systemic layer of the novel. Adaeze's investigation is no longer purely personal. She has been handed a larger pattern.
- **The lie:** *I know how this system works* is being tested. Sena knows things Adaeze doesn't, and Adaeze is not comfortable with this.

### Ch 8: Reading the Vaults
- **POV:** Adaeze, third-person limited
- **Location:** House of Adim archive; Adaeze's home
- **Save the Cat beat:** Fun and Games
- **% mark:** 28–32%
- **Emotional arc:** Methodical investigation → the specific dread of someone who is finding what they were looking for
- **Try-fail cycle:** No-and (she accesses the House of Adim's historical records and finds the pattern Sena identified — but also finds that the House of Adim's records have gaps, and the gaps are where the Grand Ledger's debt calls would be)
- **Beats:**
  1. Adaeze requests access to the House of Adim's archive — her right as a senior Assessor. She tells Obi she is doing background research for her review. This is true. It is not the whole truth. He approves the request. He straightens his desk.
  2. She spends a day in the archive. The records go back eighty years — to the Dry Decade. She maps debt calls against the pattern Sena gave her. The sequencing is visible in the House of Adim records, but incomplete. The gaps correspond to Grand Ledger clients — families wealthy enough to have accounts with the dominant House, not the minor one.
  3. She finds a reference to the margin symbol from contract seven. It appears in three other ledgers in the archive — all of them Grand Ledger clients whose debts were transferred to the House of Adim for collection. The symbol is not standard notation. It is not in any Assessor's manual she has read. She copies it into her notebook.
  4. She brings the pattern home and maps it against Sena's analysis. Together they are more complete. The sequencing is not just clustering — it is directional. The debt calls are moving through specific families, specific lineages, in a specific order. Her family is in the sequence. She is in the sequence.
  5. She reads herself again. Thin. She has been reading herself as thin for two years. She now understands that this is not stress. She has been in default since she was twenty-nine. Her body has been running on borrowed time that was never hers to spend.
- **Plants:** The three other ledgers with the margin symbol (structural — will lead to the sequencing plan in Ch 10). The directional quality of the sequence (structural — the *solvency of history* will be named in Ch 12). Adaeze's self-reading as thin, now understood as default rather than stress (action — pays off in Ch 16 when she reads herself as something different).
- **Payoffs:** The margin symbol from Ch 6 is partially resolved — it appears in multiple ledgers, confirming it is systematic rather than idiosyncratic.
- **Character movement:** Adaeze has moved from personal grief to systemic investigation. She now knows she is not an anomaly. She is a data point in a pattern.
- **The lie:** *I know how this system works* is cracking. The system is larger and older than she understood.
- **~Word count target:** 3,500

### Ch 10: Inside the Grand Ledger
- **POV:** Adaeze, third-person limited
- **Location:** Grand Ledger Quarter; Grand Ledger building (public floors)
- **Save the Cat beat:** Fun and Games
- **% mark:** 36–40%
- **Emotional arc:** Determined → the specific vertigo of an Assessor in a building full of accumulated temporal residue
- **Try-fail cycle:** No-and (she gets into the Grand Ledger's public records room and finds the sequencing notation in their published debt call records — but the deeper records are sealed, and she is recognized, and the visit is noted)
- **Beats:**
  1. Adaeze enters the Grand Ledger building as a certified Assessor with a legitimate review in progress. She has the right to access public records relevant to her case. The public records room is on the second floor. The vault is in the basement. She can smell the basement from the lobby — ten thousand ledgers, the accumulated base note of generations of borrowers, a smell that interferes with her readings. She reads everyone in the lobby. The readings are wrong. Too dense, too layered. She cannot separate them.
  2. In the public records room, she finds the margin symbol in three Grand Ledger debt call records from the past decade. She photographs them. A records clerk watches her. She works methodically, professionally, giving nothing away.
  3. She finds a reference to an internal document called the *Sequence Register* — mentioned in a footnote of a debt call record, the kind of footnote that assumes the reader already knows what it refers to. She asks the clerk for the Sequence Register. The clerk says it is a restricted internal document, not available under public records access. She asks what it pertains to. The clerk says: *"Actuarial planning. Long-term debt call scheduling."*
  4. On her way out she passes a senior Assessor she does not know — a man in his sixties, white clothing, very still hands. He looks at her. She reads him automatically and gets something she has never gotten before: a count she cannot place, layered over with vault residue, the reading sliding off him like water off oiled cloth. He knows she is reading him. He lets her fail. He nods, slightly, and continues walking.
  5. She is outside before she realizes: that was Nnaji. She has seen his name on documents for ten years. She has never met him. He knew who she was.
- **Plants:** The *Sequence Register* as a named document (structural — Adaeze will access it in Ch 17). Nnaji's unreadable count (action — pays off in Ch 17 when she understands why vault residue interferes with readings). The clerk's phrase *actuarial planning* as the Grand Ledger's public framing of the sequencing (dialogue).
- **Payoffs:** The margin symbol from Ch 6 and Ch 8 is now confirmed as a Grand Ledger notation appearing across multiple Houses' records. The Grand Ledger building as a destination she kept approaching is now entered.
- **Character movement:** Adaeze has been seen. The Grand Ledger knows she is investigating. The patient observation has become active monitoring.
- **The lie:** She is using her professional credentials as cover for an investigation that threatens the institution that issued those credentials. The lie has become the tool and the tool is becoming a liability.
- **~Word count target:** 3,500

### Ch 11: What Kofi Files
- **POV:** Adaeze, third-person limited
- **Location:** House of Adim; Adim District
- **Save the Cat beat:** Fun and Games / Bad Guys Close In (early)
- **% mark:** 40–44%
- **Emotional arc:** Focused investigation → betrayal, cold and specific
- **Try-fail cycle:** No-and (she discovers Kofi's monitoring reports; she also discovers that he has been softening them, which is more complicated than simple betrayal)
- **Beats:**
  1. Amara comes to Adaeze with something she found while organizing the House of Adim's inter-House correspondence files — a standard task for a junior trainee. She found a series of reports filed to the Grand Ledger by a Senior Assessor Kofi Asante, dated across the past three months, subject: *Mwenye Inherited Assessment Review, Status Update.* She hands them to Adaeze without saying anything. She has already read them.
  2. Adaeze reads the reports. They are accurate in their facts and misleading in their framing. Kofi has described her as *confused by the emotional weight of the inheritance* rather than *conducting a systematic investigation.* He has described her visits to the archive as *standard review preparation.* He has not mentioned the pattern analysis, the margin symbol, or the Grand Ledger visit. He has been protecting her, or managing her, and she cannot tell which.

### Ch 24: What Kofi Files (Reprise)

  2. Kofi comes to the House of Adim. He sits across from her desk. He says: *"I filed a report last night."* She waits. He says: *"A full one. Everything. The Sequence Register. - **Payoffs:** Kofi's ambivalence from Ch 11 resolves into a choice. His nine-year count from Ch 6 is now something he is spending consciously. Obi's desk-straightening as a tell for anxiety is now a tell for something more like hope.
- **Character movement:** Adaeze has been helped by someone she had written off. The isolation of Act II is not reversed but it is answered. She is not alone. The resolution is partial — the collection is suspended, not cancelled — and this is the correct amount of resolution for this story.
- **The lie:** The lie's final form: *I know how this system works.* She does know. She also knows it is not enough to know. You have to say it out loud, in a room, in front of witnesses.
