# THE ACCOUNTANCY OF GRIEF — CHARACTER REGISTRY

---

## IMPORTANT NOTE

The seed concept names **Adaeze Mwenye** as the protagonist. The build request references characters from a different novel (Cass Bellwright, Eddan, Perin, Maret Corda, etc.). This registry is built for **The Accountancy of Grief** and its actual cast. The character count and depth requirements from the brief are honored; the names are not, because using the wrong names would produce a document that contradicts the novel it's supposed to serve.

---

## CHARACTER REGISTRY

---

### 1. ADAEZE MWENYE
**Age:** 31 | **Role:** Protagonist, POV character | **Occupation:** Debt Assessor, House of Adim

---

**Ghost / Wound / Want / Need / Lie**

*Ghost:* At four years old, Adaeze's mother was dying of chest fever. Adaeze has no memory of this. What she has instead is the memory of her mother being *there* — present, well, making bitter-leaf soup every birthday, alive until Adaeze was twenty-six. That presence was purchased. Adaeze did not know.

*Wound:* She built her entire moral self-image on being the person who administers a necessary system fairly. She stamps the forms. She tells people their count honestly. She does not flinch. She has confused professional detachment with ethical integrity for her entire adult life, and the ledger has now made this confusion impossible to maintain.

*Lie:* "I am different from the system I work for because I am careful and honest within it."

*Want:* To renegotiate her inherited debt and survive — practically, legally, on paper. To find the clause, the loophole, the mechanism.

*Need:* To decide what she actually owes her mother, separate from what the contract says she owes — and to understand that these are different questions that cannot be resolved by the same method.

*Arc:* Negative-to-positive with genuine cost. She survives, but not cleanly. She disrupts the sequencing, which saves her life and breaks the plan, and she will spend the rest of her life not knowing what the plan would have produced. The novel refuses to tell her. She has to live with that.

---

**Three Sliders**

*Proactivity: 6/10.* She begins reactive — the ledger arrives, she opens it, the situation acts on her. But she is professionally trained to investigate, and once she starts reading, she cannot stop. Her proactivity increases across the novel as the stakes become clear. By the final act she is initiating, not responding.

*Likability: 8/10.* The reader is with her from the first page because her grief is recognizable and her competence is reassuring and her moral reckoning is honest. She is not always likable in the moment — she is sometimes cold, sometimes wrong, sometimes cruel in the particular way of people who are very good at seeing things and use that ability as a weapon. But she is trying, visibly and at cost.

*Competence: 9/10.* She is the best Assessor in the House of Adim and she knows it. Her competence is her primary character trait and her primary defense mechanism. The novel's central dramatic irony is that she is supremely competent at a job that has been lying to her.

---

**Speech Pattern**

1. *Vocabulary:* High professional register at work, drops to plain speech in private. She uses the technical language of the Houses — *natural yield, collateral, experiential interest* — with the fluency of someone who has spoken it for a decade, but in personal conversation she avoids it, sometimes conspicuously.
2. *Sentence length:* Medium to long. She completes her thoughts. She does not trail off.
3. *Contractions/formality:* Formal at work, moderately informal elsewhere. She does not use contractions in professional contexts. She does in private, but sparingly.
4. *Verbal tics:* Repeats the last phrase of a question back before answering it. Pauses before delivering bad news — not from reluctance, from precision. She is making sure she has the right number before she says it.
5. *Question ratio:* Low. She states. When she asks questions, they are specific and she already suspects the answer.
6. *Interruption patterns:* Does not interrupt. Waits. This reads as patience; it is partly patience and partly the habit of an Assessor who knows that people reveal more if you let them finish.
7. *Metaphor domain:* Accounting, water, weight. Things that accumulate. Things that can be measured.
8. *Directness:* Direct professionally, indirect personally. She can tell a stranger they have eight years left without flinching. She cannot tell someone she loves them without it costing her something visible.

*Example lines:*
> "Your count is good. Twenty-three years, give or take a season. That's my certification. You can take it to any House in the city."

> "I'm not asking you to explain the contract. I'm asking you to explain why my name is in it."

> "She made that soup every year. I thought it was a birthday tradition. I thought she just — I thought she liked the taste."

---

**Physical Appearance**

Tall, with the particular posture of someone who has spent years at a desk and corrects for it consciously. Dark skin, close-cropped hair she keeps oiled. A small scar at the corner of her left eye from a childhood fall she doesn't remember. Her hands are her most expressive feature — she keeps them still when she is reading someone, and people who know what that stillness means find it unsettling.

---

**Physical Habits and Tells**

- When she is reading someone's count, she goes very still and her nostrils flare slightly. She does not know she does this.
- She touches the left side of her ribcage when she is thinking about her own count. A checking gesture, like feeling for a wallet.
- She eats standing up when she is working through a problem. She has no memory of sitting down to a meal alone.
- When she is about to say something she knows will hurt someone, she looks at her hands first.

---

**Secrets**

*Secret 1:* She has known something was wrong with her own count for two years. She read herself as thin — the Assessor's word for a count that is running out — and told herself it was stress. She did not report this to the House, which she was required to do. She has been falsifying her own self-assessments on her annual certification forms. This is a crime.

*Secret 2:* She has, in the past three years, quietly adjusted the assessments of four borrowers in the Adim district — added a year or two to their certified counts so they could qualify for slightly better loan terms. She told herself this was within the margin of error. It was not within the margin of error. She has been doing the thing she condemns the Grand Ledger for doing, at a smaller scale, with better intentions, and the intentions do not change what it is.

---

**Key Relationships**

- *Chidinma Mwenye (dead mother):* The novel's central relationship, conducted entirely through the ledger. Adaeze is in conversation with a woman she cannot confront, cannot forgive, cannot stop loving.
- *Obi Adim-Eze (director, House of Adim):* Professional trust, personal wariness. She respects him. She has begun to understand that his not-knowing is a choice, and she does not know yet whether she can forgive that.
- *Sena Tawiah (Debtors' Assembly):* Mutual respect, political tension. Sena wants Adaeze's Assessor credentials for the Assembly's case. Adaeze is not ready to be used.
- *Kofi Asante (Grand Ledger Assessor):* The colleague who will become the antagonist's instrument. They trained together. She trusts him more than she should.

---

**Thematic Role**

Adaeze is the question the novel asks: *Can you be a fair functionary of an unfair system?* Her arc is the answer the novel refuses to make easy: no, but the alternative is not simple either.

### 2. CHIDINMA MWENYE
**Age:** 54 at death | **Role:** Absent presence, the mother | **Occupation:** Cloth merchant

*Chidinma does not appear in the present tense of the novel. She appears in the ledger — in contracts, in Assessor notations, in the marginal handwriting of a woman who understood, toward the end, what she had done and could not undo it. She is the most important character in the book and she never speaks a word of dialogue.*

---

**Ghost / Wound / Want / Need / Lie**

*Ghost:* At twenty-three, dying of chest fever with a four-year-old daughter and no family money, she signed the first contract. She understood the terms imperfectly. She understood that she was choosing Adaeze's future over her own life, and she chose Adaeze.

*Wound:* She spent twenty-seven years knowing what she had done and being unable to tell her daughter. Every birthday soup was an apology she could not make in words.

*Lie:* "She will have a good life, and that is worth more than the autonomy I took from her."

*Want:* To have been right. To have given Adaeze something worth the cost.

*Need:* To have told her. The ledger is the closest she got.

*Arc:* Completed before the novel begins. Tragic. She was not wrong that Adaeze needed her alive. She was not right that the choice was hers to make alone.

---

**Three Sliders**

*Proactivity: 9/10.* She acted. Decisively, repeatedly, over decades. She kept borrowing to stay ahead of the interest. She was the most active person in the story and she is dead.

*Likability: 7/10.* The reader's sympathy for her must be earned and complicated simultaneously. She is not a villain. She is a mother who made a choice that was an act of love and an act of theft, and the novel holds both without resolution.

*Competence: 6/10.* She was a competent merchant. She was not competent at understanding compound interest, or at navigating the Houses, or at finding another way. She did not know another way existed. The system was designed so she wouldn't.

---

**Physical Habits and Tells (reconstructed from ledger and memory)**

Adaeze knows her mother's hands — calloused from cloth work, always slightly stained with indigo. She knows her mother's smell, which is now the smell of the ledger. She knows that her mother made bitter-leaf soup on her birthday every year and that she hummed while she cooked and that she never talked about money.

---

**Secrets (revealed through ledger)**

*Secret 1:* The borrowing did not stop at the first contract. There were seven contracts over twenty-seven years. Each one extended Chidinma's life and deepened Adaeze's debt. The last contract was signed eleven years ago, when the chest fever returned. Chidinma knew, by then, exactly what she was doing. She signed anyway.

*Secret 2:* In the final ledger entry, in her own handwriting in the margin — not a contract clause, not an official notation, just a note — Chidinma wrote: *She will be angry. She should be angry. Tell her I knew.* The Assessor who processed the death did not remove this. Whether this was oversight or mercy is not determinable.

---

**Thematic Role**

Chidinma is the novel's unanswerable question made flesh. She is proof that love and theft can be the same act, performed with full devotion, and that this fact does not resolve into either absolution or condemnation.

### 3. OBI ADIM-EZE
**Age:** 58 | **Role:** Director, House of Adim; mentor figure | **Occupation:** Ledger House director, fourth generation

---

**Ghost / Wound / Want / Need / Lie**

*Ghost:* Fifteen years ago, he found an anomaly in the Grand Ledger's inter-House accounting — a pattern of debt calls that didn't make financial sense. He spent three months trying to understand it. Then he stopped. He has not looked at that file since.

*Wound:* He knows something is wrong and has chosen not to know what it is. He has organized his entire professional life around not-knowing, which requires constant, exhausting maintenance.

*Lie:* "The House of Adim does enough good that the larger question doesn't need to be my question."

*Want:* For Adaeze to close the ledger and let the matter rest. He does not say this. He says he wants to help her.

*Need:* To look at the file. To let someone else's courage make his own possible.

*Arc:* Supporting arc, partial positive. He does not become a hero. He gives Adaeze one crucial piece of information — the file, eventually, when he can no longer justify withholding it — and this is the most he can manage, and it costs him something real.

---

**Three Sliders**

*Proactivity: 3/10.* He is constitutionally reactive. He has built a life on not initiating.

*Likability: 7/10.* He is genuinely kind, genuinely competent at running a House that does less harm than it could. His cowardice is understandable. It is still cowardice.

*Competence: 8/10.* Excellent administrator. Skilled at navigating the political relationship with the Grand Ledger. Good at protecting his staff within the limits he has set for himself.

---

**Speech Pattern**

1. *Vocabulary:* Institutional, careful. He speaks in the language of policy and precedent.
2. *Sentence length:* Long. He qualifies. He adds clauses.
3. *Contractions:* Uses them, but formally — *it's, that's* — not *gonna, wanna*.
4. *Verbal tics:* "In my experience" before anything he is not sure about. "The Accords provide" before anything he is sure about.
5. *Question ratio:* Medium. He asks questions to redirect, not to learn.
6. *Interruption:* Rarely interrupts but often finishes sentences — not rudely, just the habit of someone who has had many conversations and knows where they go.
7. *Metaphor domain:* Architecture, foundations, load-bearing walls. Things that hold weight.
8. *Directness:* Indirect. He communicates concern through procedure.

*Example lines:*
> "The Accords provide a sixty-day review period for inherited assessments. I'd encourage you to use it. There's no requirement that you act immediately."

> "In my experience, the ledgers that are hardest to read are the ones where the borrower loved someone very much."

> "I'm not telling you to stop. I'm telling you what you'll find if you don't."

---

**Physical Appearance**

Heavyset, with the careful movements of a man who has learned to take up less space than his body suggests. Grey at his temples. Wears the same style of embroidered kaftan every day in different colors — it functions as a uniform, a way of being recognizable and therefore predictable. His office smells strongly of the contract ink, which he no longer notices.

---

**Physical Habits and Tells**

- Straightens objects on his desk when he is uncomfortable. His desk is always very straight.
- When he is about to not-tell someone something, he asks them if they want tea.
- He has a habit of looking at the door when conversations approach the topic he has spent fifteen years avoiding.

---

**Secrets**

*Secret 1:* The file he stopped reading fifteen years ago contains the earliest legible record of the sequencing plan. He does not know this. He stopped before he got there. He has told himself he stopped because the anomaly was probably a clerical error.

*Secret 2:* He has recommended Adaeze for promotion three times and been quietly blocked by the Grand Ledger each time. He has not told her this. He has told her the timing wasn't right.

---

### 4. SENA TAWIAH
**Age:** 47 | **Role:** Leader, Debtors' Assembly; ally/complication | **Occupation:** Former fishmonger, full-time agitator

---

**Ghost / Wound / Want / Need / Lie**

*Ghost:* Her daughter inherited Sena's mother's debt at age twelve and was assessed by a Grand Ledger Assessor who certified her count at nine years. The daughter is now twenty-one. She has eight months left. The assessment was accurate. Sena borrowed against her own remaining years to fund the Assembly because she decided that if she was going to spend her count, she would spend it on this.

*Wound:* She has already grieved her daughter's death. She is living past the point where hope was possible, which has made her fearless and has also made her, occasionally, ruthless in ways she doesn't always examine.

*Lie:* "The personal is finished. What matters now is the systemic."

*Want:* Jubilee. Formal invocation of the forgiveness clause. She wants the legal mechanism; she wants the Houses to be forced to honor what they promised.

*Need:* To let herself grieve while her daughter is still alive. She has been so focused on the structural that she has stopped being present for the personal, which is the same mistake the system makes, and she knows this and cannot stop.

*Arc:* Flat arc with one crack. She does not change her goals. She changes one thing: she goes home. The novel's most quietly devastating scene is Sena sitting with her daughter for an afternoon and not talking about debt.

---

**Three Sliders**

*Proactivity: 10/10.* She is the most proactive character in the novel. She has been acting for twenty years.

*Likability: 7/10.* Enormously sympathetic in her cause, difficult in person. She is not cruel, but she is transactional in a way that comes from having spent her emotional reserves.

*Competence: 8/10.* Politically sophisticated. She knows the Accords better than most lawyers. She is very good at organizing people who have been trained by the system to feel powerless.

---

**Speech Pattern**

1. *Vocabulary:* Plain, precise, occasionally formal when quoting legal text — she has memorized sections of the Orokwe Accords and deploys them without warning.
2. *Sentence length:* Short to medium. She does not waste words.
3. *Contractions:* Yes, freely.
4. *Verbal tics:* "The Accords say" (she and Obi use the same source for opposite purposes). Addresses people by their full name when she wants their full attention.
5. *Question ratio:* Low. She states. Her questions are rhetorical.
6. *Interruption:* Interrupts when she thinks someone is about to say something false. Does not apologize for it.
7. *Metaphor domain:* Bodies, hunger, weather. Physical experience. Things you feel in your body.
8. *Directness:* Very direct. Considers indirectness a luxury she cannot afford.

*Example lines:*
> "Adaeze Mwenye. You've been stamping forms for ten years. I'm asking you to read one."

> "The Accords say Jubilee. They say it in plain language. The Houses have spent four hundred years building a room around that sentence so no one can reach it. You're standing in that room."

> "I'm not asking you to trust me. I'm asking you to do the math."

---

**Physical Appearance**

Short, with the kind of stillness that reads as size. Her hair is grown out and wrapped — she stopped cutting it when she started the Assembly, a private ritual no one has asked about. Her hands are fishmonger's hands still: strong, scarred, smelling faintly of salt even years later. She wears no jewelry. She sold it.

---

**Physical Habits and Tells**

- Stands when everyone else is sitting. Not for dominance — she is more comfortable on her feet.
- When she is calculating, her lips move slightly, like she is reciting numbers.
- She does not touch people unless she means it. When she does, it lands.

---

**Secrets**

*Secret 1:* She has already found the buried exception in the Orokwe Accords — the one that allows a Ledger House to lend to someone with zero or negative natural yield. She has not told Adaeze because she is not sure whether it is a lifeline or a trap, and she needs Adaeze's Assessor expertise to determine which.

*Secret 2:* She has been offered a private settlement by the Grand Ledger — her daughter's debt cleared, in exchange for dissolving the Assembly. She has not told anyone. She has not accepted. She has not refused. The letter has been sitting in her bag for six weeks.

---

### 5. KOFI ASANTE
**Age:** 34 | **Role:** Grand Ledger Assessor; colleague, false ally | **Occupation:** Senior Assessor, Grand Ledger

---

**Ghost / Wound / Want / Need / Lie**

*Ghost:* Kofi and Adaeze trained together. He was better than her at the technical work; she was better at the readings. He went to the Grand Ledger because the pay was better and the clients were easier and he told himself the institution was the institution and you worked within it or you didn't work. He has been telling himself this for nine years.

*Wound:* He is aware of the sequencing. Not the full shape of it — he is not senior enough — but he has been assigned to monitor Adaeze's case since she opened the ledger, and he knows what *patient observation* means, and he has not told her.

*Lie:* "I am protecting her by managing this quietly."

*Want:* For Adaeze to stop investigating before she forces him to act against her.

*Need:* To tell her the truth before it is too late for either of them.

*Arc:* Negative arc with a last-act choice. He is not a villain; he is a man who has made a series of small accommodations that have added up to complicity, and when the moment comes he either finds something left in himself or he doesn't. The novel decides this at the last possible moment.

---

**Three Sliders**

*Proactivity: 4/10.* He acts when required. He prefers not to be required.

*Likability: 6/10.* Genuinely warm, genuinely funny, genuinely compromised. The reader likes him and is not sure they should.

*Competence: 8/10.* Excellent Assessor. Has been absorbing Grand Ledger vault residue for nine years and his readings have shifted in ways he has attributed to professional development.

---

**Speech Pattern**

1. *Vocabulary:* Professional but warmer than Adaeze's — he uses the technical language but wraps it in social ease.
2. *Sentence length:* Medium, with frequent asides. He talks the way a person talks when they are slightly nervous and covering it with friendliness.
3. *Contractions:* Freely, even at work.
4. *Verbal tics:* Laughs at his own observations before finishing them. Uses "honestly" before things that are not fully honest.
5. *Question ratio:* High. He asks questions to seem engaged and to deflect.
6. *Interruption:* Interrupts with additions, not corrections — "yes, and—" rather than "no, but—"
7. *Metaphor domain:* Games, navigation, weather. Things with rules you can learn.
8. *Directness:* Indirect, pleasantly. He is very good at saying nothing while appearing to say something.

*Example lines:*
> "Honestly, the inherited debt cases are the worst. I always say — not that anyone asks — I always say the Accords should have put a cap on inheritance. But here we are."

> "You look tired, Ada. Have you been sleeping? Stupid question. Of course you haven't."

> "I'm not telling you to drop it. I'm just — I'm saying, there are people at the Grand Ledger who are paying attention to this. That's all I'm saying."

---

**Physical Appearance**

Medium height, with an easy physical confidence — he moves like someone who has never had to make himself small. Keeps his beard very neat. His clothes are better than an Assessor's salary justifies; he has a Grand Ledger clothing allowance he does not mention.

---

**Secrets**

*Secret 1:* He has been filing monitoring reports on Adaeze's investigation to the Grand Ledger for three months. He has been softening them — describing her as confused rather than dangerous, buying her time he does not know he is buying.

*Secret 2:* His own count. He had himself assessed by an independent reader in the mangrove margin two years ago — illegally, using a contact he has never disclosed. The reading was not good. He has nine years. He has not borrowed against them. He does not know why not, exactly. He thinks it might be the only honest thing he has done recently.

### 6. THE LEDGER-MASTER EMEKA NNAJI
**Age:** 71 | **Role:** Senior Ledger-Master, Grand Ledger; institutional antagonist | **Occupation:** Third-generation Ledger-Master

---

**Ghost / Wound / Want / Need / Lie**

*Ghost:* He inherited the plan at forty-two, when his father died and left him the senior position and a private ledger — not a debt ledger, a *plan ledger* — that laid out the sequencing in full. He spent two years trying to find a flaw in it. He could not. He has been executing it since.

*Wound:* He has called in debts that took specific people's specific experiences — a woman's memory of her son's first words, a man's experience of recovery from illness — and he has done this believing it was necessary, and he has been right that it was necessary according to the plan, and he has not let himself examine whether the plan is right.

*Lie:* "The solvency of history is worth the cost. The founders knew what they were doing."

*Want:* The sequencing to complete. Adaeze to die on schedule, quietly, in the manner the contract specifies.

*Need:* To ask whether the founders' plan — made four hundred years ago by people who are dead, based on calculations that assumed compliance — should be executed by people who have never been permitted to evaluate it.

*Arc:* Flat arc that cracks once, too late. He is not redeemable in the novel's timeframe. He is understandable.

---

**Three Sliders**

*Proactivity: 7/10.* He has been executing a plan for thirty years. He is proactive in the sense that a machine is proactive — consistently, without deviation.

*Likability: 4/10.* The reader can understand him. Understanding is not the same as sympathy.

*Competence: 9/10.* He is very good at this. That is the problem.

---

**Speech Pattern**

1. *Vocabulary:* Archaic formal. He uses the language of the original Accords — terms that are four hundred years old, deployed with the ease of a man who has read nothing else.
2. *Sentence length:* Long, structured, with the rhythm of legal documents read aloud.
3. *Contractions:* Never.
4. *Verbal tics:* "The Accords provide" (same phrase as Obi, different valence — Obi uses it for protection, Nnaji uses it as a door closing). "In the matter of" before every specific reference.
5. *Question ratio:* Very low. He does not ask. He informs.
6. *Interruption:* Does not interrupt. Waits with complete stillness, then speaks as if the other person has not.
7. *Metaphor domain:* Mathematics, settlement, finality. Things that resolve.
8. *Directness:* Absolutely direct about everything except the plan's purpose. He will tell you exactly what is happening. He will not tell you why.

*Example lines:*
> "In the matter of the Mwenye inheritance: the debt is valid, the contract is binding, and the period of patient observation has concluded. The House extends its condolences for your loss."

> "The Accords provide for exactly this circumstance. The founders were thorough."

> "I am not asking you to understand. I am asking you to honor the contract your mother signed."

---

**Physical Appearance**

Tall, thin in the way of someone who has been thin their whole life and is now elderly and thinner still. Wears white exclusively — not as a statement, as a habit so old it has become invisible to him. His hands are very steady. His eyes are the eyes of an Assessor who has spent forty years in the vault district: slightly unfocused, reading something that isn't there.

---

**Secrets**

*Secret 1:* He does not know what the solvency of history produces. His father did not know. His grandfather did not know. The plan ledger describes the sequencing in precise detail and describes the outcome in a single phrase that no one in three generations has been able to interpret. He has been executing a plan toward an end he cannot read, on faith in people who are four hundred years dead. He has never admitted this to anyone.

*Secret 2:* Two of the three Assessors who died in the past three years were not natural deaths. He authorized the collections himself. He recorded them as standard experiential interest payments. He sleeps without difficulty.

---

**Thematic Role**

Nnaji is the answer to the question *what does the system believe?* He is not a hypocrite. He believes in the plan with the same completeness that Chidinma believed in Adaeze's future. The novel draws this parallel deliberately and without comfort.

### 7. AMARA NWOSU
**Age:** 19 | **Role:** Junior Assessor trainee, House of Adim; Adaeze's structural mirror | **Occupation:** Apprentice Assessor

*Amara is the character Adaeze was at nineteen — talented, idealistic, beginning to understand what the job requires — except that Amara is a third-generation debtor who knows it, whose grandmother borrowed in the Dry Decade, whose mother borrowed to pay the interest on that, and who is training to be an Assessor in part because she wants to understand the system that has been eating her family for eighty years. She is the novel's future tense.*

---

**Ghost / Wound / Want / Need / Lie**

*Ghost:* At fourteen, she watched her mother receive an Assessor's visit — a debt call, an experiential interest collection. Her mother came back from that appointment and could describe the room where her brother's naming ceremony had been held but could not remember being happy there. The hollow feeling, witnessed from outside.

*Wound:* She is training inside the system that damaged her family because she cannot think of another way to fight it, and she is not sure whether she is a spy or a collaborator and the uncertainty is constant.

*Lie:* "Knowledge is neutral. Learning how this works doesn't make me part of it."

*Want:* To become good enough at assessment to protect people — to read counts accurately and give borrowers honest information before they sign.

*Need:* To understand that knowledge inside the system is not neutral, and to decide what she will do with that understanding before it is made for her.

*Arc:* Beginning arc — the novel ends before she has resolved it. She is the question the next generation will answer.

---

**Three Sliders**

*Proactivity: 5/10.* Reactive now, proactive later. The novel catches her at the inflection point.

*Likability: 9/10.* She is young and trying and already carrying more than she should have to carry.

*Competence: 6/10, rising.* She has the gift — Adaeze recognized it in her first assessment exercise. She is not yet trained enough to be dangerous.

---

**Speech Pattern**

1. *Vocabulary:* Mixed — she has the technical language from training, the plain speech of the Adim district, and the occasional formal phrasing of someone who has been reading legal documents to understand them rather than to use them.
2. *Sentence length:* Short to medium. She has not yet developed the professional long sentence.
3. *Contractions:* Freely, except when nervous, when she goes formal.
4. *Verbal tics:* "Is that—" before questions she already knows the answer to and is hoping she's wrong. Apologizes for observations as she makes them.
5. *Question ratio:* High. She is still learning.
6. *Interruption:* Doesn't interrupt senior staff. Interrupts herself.
7. *Metaphor domain:* Hands, tools, making things. She grew up watching her mother weave.
8. *Directness:* Trying to be direct, not yet comfortable with it. Her directness arrives in bursts, then retreats.

*Example lines:*
> "Is that — I'm sorry, is that what experiential interest actually means? They take a memory? Not just time?"

> "I read her as about forty years. Is that — I know that's not right. I know she's thirty-one. But that's what I read."

> "My grandmother borrowed in the Dry Decade. I know that. I've always known that. I didn't know there was still a ledger."

---

**Physical Habits and Tells**

- Writes everything down. Has three notebooks in her bag at all times.
- When she is doing a reading, she holds her breath. Adaeze has been trying to train her out of this.
- Looks at Adaeze the way Adaeze probably looked at her first mentor — with the particular attention of someone who is learning how to be a person from watching another person be one.

---

**Secrets**

*Secret 1:* She has already read her own family's ledger — she found it in her mother's things two years ago and has been working through it alone, without telling anyone, because she did not know there was anyone to tell. She has partial information and incorrect conclusions and she is one conversation with Adaeze away from understanding what she is actually looking at.

*Secret 2:* Her count. She has not assessed herself — she is not yet certified to certify — but she has tried, the way trainees try when they think no one is watching. She could not read herself clearly. She does not know if this means she has a long count or if it means she has her mother's hollow feeling and cannot feel the edges of herself anymore.

---

**Thematic Role**

Amara is the cost made visible and the future made possible. She is what the system produces — a person shaped by debt who is trying to become someone who can change it — and whether that is possible is the question the novel leaves open when it ends.
