# CHARACTERS.MD
## The Accountancy of Grief — Character Registry

---

> **Compiler's Note:** The names below diverge from the seed document's placeholder names (Cass Bellwright, Eddan, etc.) because those belong to a different story. This registry is built for *The Accountancy of Grief* and its actual cast. All characters are grounded in Varanthe, the Seru delta, and the temporal debt system as specified.

---

## 1. ADAEZE MWENYE
**Age:** 31 | **Role:** Protagonist, POV character | **Occupation:** Debt-Assessor, Minor Ledger House (House Obuya)

### Ghost / Wound / Want / Need / Lie

**Ghost:** At age four, Adaeze's mother Amina contracted a fever that the neighborhood healer said would kill her within the season. Adaeze does not remember this. She remembers only a mother who was present, competent, warm — who braided her hair and corrected her arithmetic and smelled of shea and river clay. The fever is a story she was told once and filed away. She has no emotional access to it.

**Wound:** She has spent her adult life being the person who tells other people how much time they have left. She is fluent in the language of finitude. What she cannot do — what she has never been able to do — is apply that fluency to herself. She assesses everyone except herself. She knows exactly how much time a fisherman has when he sits across her desk. She has never once asked the question about her own body.

**Lie:** *The system is hard but fair, and I am one of its fair parts.*

**Want:** To renegotiate the debt. To find a contractual exit. To solve this the way she solves everything — through careful reading, correct procedure, the right clause invoked at the right moment.

**Need:** To stop administering the system from inside it and reckon with what the system actually is — not a neutral machine she operates, but the mechanism that consumed her mother and is consuming her.

**Truth (opposes Lie):** The system is not fair. She has never been a fair part of it. She has been its instrument, and the people she assessed fairly were still destroyed fairly.

### Three Sliders
- **Proactivity: 7** — She initiates. She reads the ledger when she could have put it away. She pulls the irregular files when she could have left them alone. But her proactivity is procedural — she acts through investigation, not confrontation, and she is slow to act when action requires her to break a rule she believes in.
- **Likability: 8** — She is competent without being cold, principled without being preachy, and her grief is legible without being performed. Readers will recognize her particular mode of coping: staying busy, staying useful, not looking directly at the thing.
- **Competence: 9** — She is genuinely excellent at her job. This is load-bearing. Her competence means she cannot claim ignorance of what the system does — she has seen it do it, case by case, for eight years.

**Arc Type:** Negative-to-Positive (she loses her false security and gains true sight). The arc is not triumphant. She survives, but what she survives into is not comfort.

### Speech Pattern
1. **Vocabulary:** Professional, precise. She uses technical language naturally — *collateral, yield, compounding, principal* — not to impress but because these are genuinely how she thinks.
2. **Sentence length:** Medium-long in explanation, very short when she's frightened or angry. Her fear sounds like clipped bureaucratic notation.
3. **Contractions/formality:** Moderately formal. She uses contractions in casual speech but reverts to full constructions when she's hiding emotion.
4. **Verbal tics:** Begins answers with a beat of silence. Often says *"Let me read that again"* when she needs time to think, even when there's nothing in front of her to read.
5. **Question ratio:** Low. She makes statements. When she asks questions, they are specific and unanswerable — *"What did she think I would do with this?"*
6. **Interruption:** Rarely interrupts. When she does, it's to correct a factual error, not to insert her feelings.
7. **Metaphor domain:** Accounting, water, weight. Things are *in arrears*, *underwater*, *bearing more than the structure was built for*.
8. **Directness:** Direct about facts, oblique about feelings. She will tell you exactly what the contract says. She will not tell you what it means to her.

**Example lines:**
- *"The clause is unambiguous. What you're asking me is whether the clause is just, and that's a different question, and it's not one I'm authorized to answer."*
- *"She borrowed seventy-three years. I've been checking the arithmetic for four days. It doesn't change."*
- *"I'm not angry. I'm — let me read that again."*

### Physical Description
Compact, deliberate in movement. She carries herself like someone who has learned to take up exactly the space she needs and no more. Dark skin, close-cropped hair she keeps practical, not styled. Her eyes are the thing — the assessment gift makes them slightly uncanny, a fraction too steady when she looks at you, like she's reading something behind your face. She wears the same three blouses in rotation. Her hands are ink-stained on the right, always.

### Physical Habits and Tells
- When she's processing something painful, she straightens objects near her — a cup, a stack of papers — small acts of order.
- She holds her breath during difficult assessments and exhales only when she's written the number down.
- She rubs the pad of her right thumb across her left palm when she's lying, a gesture so habitual she no longer knows she does it.

### Secrets
1. She has known for two years that her assessments of House Obuya's poorest clients have been systematically low — she's been quietly giving people more time than she officially records. She told herself it was rounding error. It was not.
2. She assessed her own mother once, seven years ago, at a routine renewal. She looked at her and knew. She wrote down a different number. She has never told anyone this and has spent seven years not thinking about it.

### Key Relationships
- **Amina Mwenye (dead mother):** The entire novel. Love and theft and the impossibility of separating them.
- **Kofi Asante (colleague, see below):** The only person who knows how good she is. Also the only person who might guess what she's been doing with the numbers.
- **Senior Ledger-Master Yewande Osa:** The institutional antagonist. Adaeze has always respected her. This will not survive the novel.

## 2. AMINA MWENYE
**Age at death:** 54 | **Role:** Dead mother, ghost in the text | **Occupation:** Former schoolteacher

She does not appear except in the ledger, in memory, and in what Adaeze pieces together. She must be as fully realized as any living character because the novel's central question is whether Adaeze can hold two true things about her simultaneously.

### Ghost / Wound / Want / Need / Lie

**Ghost:** The fever at twenty-three. The Assessor who looked at her and told her she had eighteen months. Her daughter was four and did not yet know how to read. Amina walked to House Obuya the same afternoon.

**Wound:** She spent the rest of her life knowing she had purchased it. Every good day was a line item. She became, over time, a person who could not receive love without immediately calculating its cost.

**Lie:** *A mother's first obligation is to be present. Everything else is secondary.*

**Want:** To give Adaeze a complete childhood. To be there for it.

**Need:** To tell Adaeze what she had done, and let Adaeze decide what to do with that knowledge.

**Truth:** Presence purchased through theft is not presence — it is a debt she passed on without consent, and Adaeze deserved to know.

### What the Ledger Reveals
The ledger is not a villain's document. It is a desperate woman's document. The early entries are terrified — the handwriting shakes in the margins where the borrower can annotate. By the time Adaeze is ten, the handwriting steadies. By the time Adaeze is sixteen, there are no annotations at all. Amina stopped arguing with herself. She made her peace with what she'd done and loved her daughter with the time she'd bought, and that is both the most human thing in the book and the thing Adaeze cannot forgive and cannot condemn.

### Speech (reconstructed through memory)
Adaeze remembers her mother's speech as warm, slightly formal, teacher's cadences — full sentences, gentle corrections, a habit of asking *"What do you think?"* before giving her own opinion. In the ledger's margins, she writes in fragments. Short, declarative, stripped of the warmth. The contrast is the wound.

## 3. YEWANDE OSA
**Age:** 61 | **Role:** Senior Ledger-Master, House Obuya / institutional antagonist | **Occupation:** Administrator, architect of the sequencing

### Ghost / Wound / Want / Need / Lie

**Ghost:** Yewande inherited her own debt at nineteen — her father's, modest but real. She paid it back in eleven years through exceptional work and absolute loyalty to the Ledger House system. She has never forgotten those eleven years. She has also never acknowledged that paying back her debt required her to help collect everyone else's.

**Wound:** She has converted her survival into ideology. The system saved her (she believes). The system is therefore worth preserving. People who threaten the system are threatening the thing that saved her.

**Lie:** *The sequencing is a necessary correction. Someone has to hold the structure together.*

**Want:** To bring the sequencing to completion — to achieve what she calls "the solvency of history" in approximately thirty years, which she believes will permanently stabilize temporal debt and end the cycle of intergenerational extraction.

**Need:** To reckon with the fact that she is doing to others exactly what was done to her, and calling it salvation.

### Three Sliders
- **Proactivity: 9** — She has been running this plan for twenty years. She is not reactive. She is the thing others react to.
- **Likability: 5** — She is not a monster. She is terrifyingly reasonable. She has answers for every objection. Her care for the institution is genuine. Her blindness to what the institution does is also genuine. Readers will understand her and not forgive her.
- **Competence: 10** — She is the most competent person in the novel. This is the problem.

**Arc Type:** Flat — she does not change. Her function is to show what Adaeze could become if she stays inside the system long enough.

### Speech Pattern
1. **Vocabulary:** Elevated, precise, with the particular warmth of someone who has learned to deliver bad news kindly.
2. **Sentence length:** Long, structured, with subordinate clauses that absorb objections before they're raised.
3. **Contractions:** Rare. She speaks in full constructions always.
4. **Verbal tics:** *"You understand"* inserted mid-sentence, not as a question — as an assumption of agreement. *"The contract has always been clear"* as a closing move.
5. **Question ratio:** She asks questions rhetorically. She does not actually want answers.
6. **Interruption:** She does not interrupt. She waits, and then she responds to what you should have said rather than what you said.
7. **Metaphor domain:** Architecture, foundations, load-bearing. She talks about the system the way engineers talk about bridges.
8. **Directness:** Indirect about intent, direct about consequence. She will not tell you what she's doing. She will tell you exactly what will happen to you.

**Example lines:**
- *"Adaeze. You are one of our finest Assessors. You understand, I think, that what you're looking at is a long-term stability measure, not a cruelty."*
- *"The contract has always been clear. Your mother signed it with full knowledge of the terms. Her love for you was real. These two facts are not in conflict."*
- *"I have been patient with you because you are grieving. I will be patient with you a little longer. But you should understand: the sequencing does not wait for grief."*

### Physical Description
Tall, unhurried. She dresses in deep indigo, always. Silver at her temples that she has never tried to hide. Her hands are completely still when she talks — a stillness that reads as control rather than calm. She smells faintly of the cedar oil used to preserve the oldest ledgers.

### Secrets
1. She knows that "the solvency of history" is not a stabilization — it is a reset that will erase the debt records of the founding families and transfer the accumulated temporal wealth to the Ledger Houses as permanent endowment. The poor will not benefit. She has told herself they will.
2. She assessed Adaeze's mother herself, twenty-seven years ago. She set the interest terms. She knew what Amina's debt would do to Adaeze. She approved it anyway because Adaeze, as an inheriting debtor who would become an Assessor, was useful to the sequencing.

## 4. KOFI ASANTE
**Age:** 35 | **Role:** Adaeze's colleague and the closest thing she has to a friend | **Occupation:** Debt-Assessor, House Obuya

### Ghost / Wound / Want / Need / Lie

**Ghost:** Kofi's family paid off their debt three generations back. He has never owed the Ledger Houses anything. He took the Assessor position because it pays well and because he is good at it, and he has spent eight years not examining what that combination means.

**Wound:** He is a man of genuine conscience operating inside a machine that requires him to suppress it professionally. He has become very good at the suppression. He mistakes the suppression for neutrality.

**Lie:** *Witnessing without participating is a form of innocence.*

**Want:** For Adaeze to find a way out that doesn't require him to do anything.

**Need:** To stop being a witness and become a participant — in either direction.

### Three Sliders
- **Proactivity: 4** — He is reactive until the last third of the novel, when Adaeze's situation forces him to choose.
- **Likability: 8** — He is warm, funny, self-aware enough to know he's compromised but not yet brave enough to act on it. Readers will like him and be frustrated with him in equal measure.
- **Competence: 7** — Good at the job, not exceptional. He doesn't have Adaeze's gift.

**Arc Type:** Positive minor arc — he moves from witness to participant. It costs him his job. The novel implies it was the right cost.

### Speech Pattern
1. **Vocabulary:** Colloquial, warm. He uses the street names for things rather than the institutional terms.
2. **Sentence length:** Medium, conversational, with trailing observations — *"Which is fine. That's fine. I just think—"*
3. **Contractions:** Heavy. He speaks the way people talk, not the way people write.
4. **Verbal tics:** Ends observations with *"is all"* — *"I'm just saying it doesn't add up, is all."* Uses humor to approach things he's uncomfortable with.
5. **Question ratio:** High. He processes through questions.
6. **Interruption:** Interrupts himself more than others. Starts sentences he doesn't finish.
7. **Metaphor domain:** Food, weather, practical objects. He talks about abstract things in concrete terms.
8. **Directness:** Indirect about serious things, direct about small things. He'll tell you your collar is crooked. He will take four conversations to tell you he's worried about you.

**Example lines:**
- *"I'm not saying the numbers are wrong. I'm saying the numbers are very specifically wrong in a very specific direction, is all."*
- *"You want some of this? It's too much fish. I always order too much fish. — What did the ledger say, exactly?"*
- *"Adaeze. I've been your colleague for eight years. I know what you look like when you're fine and I know what you look like when you're — this is not fine."*

### Physical Description
Broad-shouldered, tends to fill whatever room he's in without meaning to. He has a habit of wearing his Assessor's sash incorrectly — slightly off-center — that Adaeze has corrected so many times it has become a ritual between them. Laugh lines already deep at thirty-five.

### Secrets
1. He noticed Adaeze's numbers were off eighteen months ago. He has said nothing because he didn't want to know why, and he has spent eighteen months feeling bad about that.
2. He has his own small debt — a six-month loan he took out during a bad year, never mentioned to anyone. He is terrified of what it means that he did it without thinking.

## 5. SENA DOKU
**Age:** 44 | **Role:** House Obuya's senior Assessor for the wealthy districts; secondary antagonist | **Occupation:** Assessor

Sena is what Adaeze is in danger of becoming — someone who has been inside the system long enough that the system's logic has become her own. She is not cruel. She is thorough. She has assessed five hundred people and felt nothing she couldn't file away by end of day.

### Ghost / Wound / Want / Need / Lie

**Ghost:** Her first assessment, at twenty-two, was her own grandmother. She looked at her and saw three years. She wrote it down. She went home and was sick in the street and told herself she would become someone who could do this job with dignity. She did. She has never asked what she traded for that dignity.

**Lie:** *Detachment is professionalism. Professionalism is compassion.*

**Want:** To be recognized as the best Assessor in Varanthe — which she may be.

**Need:** To feel something about what she does.

### Three Sliders
- **Proactivity: 6**
- **Likability: 4** — Not unlikable, but unsettling. She is too smooth. Her competence reads as absence.
- **Competence: 10**

### Speech Pattern
Flat affect, very precise. She does not use intensifiers — nothing is *very* or *quite* or *rather*. Things simply are or are not. She speaks in complete sentences always. She asks no questions. She makes no jokes. She is not unkind; she simply has no register for it.

**Example line:** *"Your remaining assessment is four years, two months, approximately. The contract allows for a six-month grace period. You should begin your arrangements."*

### Secrets
Her assessment gift has been degrading for two years. She can no longer see clearly past about five years of remaining life. She has been extrapolating from patterns and has not told anyone. Several of her recent assessments are wrong.

## 6. THE LEDGER ITSELF (Amina's Debt-Book)

Not a character in the conventional sense, but it functions as one — it is the novel's MacGuffin, its central text, and its moral center. It should be described with the same precision as any character.

**Physical description:** Octavo size, covers of salt-treated leather gone soft at the corners. It smells of shea oil and something underneath, something that Adaeze recognizes as her mother's particular biological smell — the smell of being held as a child — and underneath that, something older, something that smells like river clay and time. The pages are thick, cream-colored, filled with the Ledger House's official notation in black ink and her mother's annotations in the margins in blue. The annotations start dense and become sparser as the ledger progresses. The last twenty pages have no annotations at all.

**What it contains:** Seventy-three years of borrowed time, broken into eleven separate contracts across twenty-seven years. The interest terms. The experiential collateral already collected — listed in the clinical language of the House: *"One (1) instance: first experience of professional pride, borrower age 34. Collected."* Amina would have felt this as a blurring, a memory she couldn't quite locate. She would have known something had been taken. She would have kept going.

## VOICE IDENTITY (completed)

### Tone
Quiet and precise, the way grief actually sounds — not wailing, but the specific silence of someone reading a document that changes everything. There is warmth underneath the precision. The novel does not flinch from beauty or from love. It simply refuses to let either one excuse anything.

### Sentence Rhythm
Narration runs in medium-long sentences that accumulate weight through subordinate clauses — the way bureaucratic language accumulates, the way debt compounds. Action is short. Revelation is short. The most important sentences in the book are the ones with no subordinate clauses at all.

### Vocabulary Register
A blend: the technical vocabulary of debt and contract sits alongside the sensory vocabulary of the delta — fish-smell, salt-bleach, the amber of dry-season light. Neither register apologizes for the other. The world is both beautiful and transactional, and the prose holds both.

### POV and Tense
Third person limited, past tense, tight to Adaeze. Occasional free indirect discourse that blurs the line between Adaeze's thoughts and the narration — the reader should sometimes be uncertain whether a judgment belongs to Adaeze or to the novel. This uncertainty is thematic.

### Exemplar Passages

**1 — Opening register:**
> The ledger smelled like her mother. That was the first thing, before the numbers, before the date of the first contract, before the clause on page four that Adaeze read three times and then set down and walked to the window and stood there looking at the river until she could read it a fourth time. It smelled like shea oil and river clay and something older, something that had no name except *her*, and Adaeze had not expected that. She had opened ledgers every working day for eight years. She had not expected this one to smell like being carried.

**2 — Assessment scene:**
> The man across the desk was fifty-two and had the look of someone who had borrowed recently — colors slightly too vivid, movements half a beat behind intention, the particular quality of being adjacent to his own moment. Adaeze looked at him the way she had learned to look, and the number came: eleven years, perhaps twelve. She wrote eleven. She wrote it in the official column and she did not look at his face when she said it and she had not looked at anyone's face when she said it for four years, which she had always understood as professionalism and now was beginning to understand as something else.

**3 — Reading the ledger:**
> *One (1) instance: daughter's first day of school, borrower present. Collateral applied against Year 7 interest.*
>
> She had a memory of that day. Her mother's hand in hers at the school gate, the particular grip of it, the smell of her. The memory was complete. It had always been complete. She had never known it was a receipt.

**4 — Confrontation with Yewande:**
> "You approved her first contract," Adaeze said. Not a question.
>
> Yewande did not look away. She had the quality of a building that has decided not to fall. "I did."
>
> "She was twenty-three. She had a four-year-old."
>
> "Yes."
>
> "And you knew what the interest terms would produce."
>
> "I knew what they would require. I knew you would be an Assessor. I knew you would be useful." A pause. "I also knew she would have died in the spring. I knew you would have grown up without her. These are also facts."
>
> Adaeze straightened the ledger on the desk in front of her. Then she straightened it again. "You're going to tell me that makes it fair."
>
> "No," Yewande said. "I'm going to tell you that it makes it real."

### Anti-Exemplars

**Too lyrical, wrong register:**
> *The river sang its ancient song through the mangrove cathedral, each leaf a whispered prayer to the ancestors who had first woven time into the fabric of being.*

**Too modern/flat:**
> *Adaeze was basically done with this whole situation. Like, her mom had literally stolen her entire future and nobody had even mentioned it?*

**Too expository:**
> *The Ledger Houses, as Adaeze knew from her eight years of work as an Assessor, operated on a system of temporal debt that had been established in Year 0 by the Ancestors, who created the first contracts.*

**Too melodramatic:**
> *Grief tore through her like a blade. Her mother — her beloved, her north star — had been a thief. The betrayal was absolute. Nothing would ever be the same.*
