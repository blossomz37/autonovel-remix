# WORLD.MD

## Cosmology & History

### Founding Myth
In the beginning, the river Seru flowed pure and unbroken through the land, a gift of the Ancestors, who drew the first breath in the mists of dawn. They wove the threads of life, creating rhythm and time. But as they watched over their creation, they grew envious of the mortals who borrowed from the river’s flow, living lives full of joy and sorrow. The Ancestors decided to bind time with contracts, creating the first Ledger House to manage the delicate balance between life and its debts.

### Timeline of Major Events

- **Year 0**: Establishment of the first Ledger House, *Ikhonya*, by the Ancestors. The first contracts are signed, allowing mortals to borrow time against their futures.
  
- **Year 150**: The Great Collapse occurs as the debt of the first generations becomes overwhelming. The Ancestors withdraw, believing mortals have mismanaged their gifts. Time becomes a currency of grief.

- **Year 300**: The Second Ledger House is founded, *Karamor*, to help maintain stability and fairness among the growing population, but it becomes evident that inequalities emerge as wealthier families manipulate the system.

- **Year 600**: The Rise of the Dynasties leads to a division of power among Ledger Houses, each vying for control over specific neighborhoods, leading to entrenched class structures.

- **Year 900**: The *Elders’ Accord* is signed, establishing laws governing inheritance and the passing of debts, reinforcing the notion that one’s life can be collateral for another’s comfort.

- **Year 1200**: The *Reclamation Movement* begins, advocating for the rights of those burdened by inherited debts. Adaeze’s ancestors are part of this movement, seeking to reform the system that has trapped them.

- **Year 1400**: The emergence of *Sustainable Lending Practices* creates a temporary reprieve for the poor, allowing limited access to time without the burden of heavy interest. However, it is soon exploited by the wealthy.

- **Year 2000**: The death of Adaeze’s mother, *Amina Mwenye*, marks a key moment, as she leaves a heavy debt of seventy-three years to Adaeze. This act unknowingly triggers a series of events that challenge the status quo of the Ledger Houses.

### Recent Events
- **Year 2023**: Adaeze discovers her mother’s ledger, revealing the extent of the debt and the implications for her future. The *Ledger House of Minor Debt* begins to show irregular patterns in lending, hinting at a larger conspiracy among the major Ledger Houses.

## Magic System

### Hard Rules (Tonal Law)
1. **Borrowing Time**: Individuals may borrow years equivalent to their natural lifespan minus the years already lived. If a person is 30, they may borrow up to 50 years. However, the contract must be signed in the presence of an Assessor.
   - **Cost**: Each year borrowed adds layers of *experiential interest*, which are drawn from the borrower’s future experiences, chosen by the Ledger House.
   
2. **Inherited Debt**: Upon the death of a debtor, their debt automatically transfers to their named heir, who must honor the contract signed by their predecessor.
   - **Cost**: Upon inheriting debt, the heir must now live with the knowledge that their life was purchased at the expense of their parent’s future.
   
3. **Assessment**: Assessors possess the ability to perceive a person's remaining life span by making eye contact. This is an intuitive, non-verbal communication of life force.
   - **Cost**: Assessors often become detached from emotional bonds, as their job requires them to view lives as numbers on a ledger.

### Soft Magic (Cass's Gift)
The soft magic manifests in the form of *time whispers*, subtle echoes of past lives experienced by those burdened with debt. These whispers are felt by the borrower as a haunting presence, often causing confusion and disorientation. Each whisper carries the weight of an experience that once existed, now claimed by the Ledger House.
- **Cost**: Those who hear these whispers often find themselves losing touch with reality, as the memories of experiences that did not belong to them begin to blend with their own.

### Societal Implications
- **Governance**: The Ledger Houses operate almost like a government, enforcing laws around borrowing and inheritance. Those in power tend to come from families with the least debt, creating a cycle of privilege.
  
- **Commerce**: The economy is heavily influenced by time credits, as businesses operate on borrowed years, making it difficult for small enterprises to thrive due to interest payments.
  
- **Education**: Access to education is stratified; wealthier families can buy time for their children, allowing them greater opportunities. In contrast, poorer families struggle to educate their children, who are often burdened with debts before they even begin their lives.

## Geography

### Varanthe
Varanthe is a sprawling city, built on stilts above the delta waters of the Seru River. Its architecture is a blend of woven palm fronds and mud bricks, creating a living structure that breathes with the tides.

- **District of Akwasi**: Known for its vibrant markets filled with dried fish, spices, and textiles. The air is thick with the scent of palm kernel smoke, punctuated by the cries of vendors.
  
- **District of Iya**: A quieter area where the wealthy reside. Homes are adorned with intricate carvings, and gardens filled with exotic plants. The air carries the fragrance of blooming hibiscus and the sound of distant laughter.

- **District of Oti**: A neighborhood often overlooked, populated by laborers and those in debt. The salty air mingles with the smell of the river and the sounds of children playing in the streets, often scavenging for food.

### Neighboring Locations
- **The Hushed Marshes**: To the north, dense mangroves conceal secrets of lost debts and forgotten lives. Visitors report hearing whispers among the trees, as if the spirits of the past linger.
  
- **The Crystal Coves**: South of Varanthe, these coves are known for their clear waters and vibrant marine life. They serve as a retreat for the wealthy, but are viewed as a luxury by those who struggle.

## Factions & Politics

### The Major Ledger Houses
1. **Ikhonya**: The oldest and most powerful, they hold significant influence over the economy and politics, often exploiting their position to maintain control over the lower classes.
  
2. **Karamor**: Known for their ethical lending practices, they are seen as a counterbalance to Ikhonya’s tyranny. However, their methods often lead to complications as they navigate the interests of the wealthy and the desperate.

### The Reclamation Movement
A grassroots organization formed by those burdened by debt, advocating for fair treatment and reform in the borrowing system. They believe in the right to a future free from inherited debt.

### The Elders’ Council
A council of respected community leaders and former Assessor veterans. They are seen as a moderating force but are often criticized for their complacency in the face of systemic issues.

## Bestiary / Flora / Natural World

### Unique Flora
- **Timeweaver Vines**: These creeping plants are said to absorb the residual energy from borrowed time, growing stronger with each debt incurred in their vicinity. Their flowers bloom in vibrant hues but emit a faint, disorienting hum.

### Unique Fauna
- **Whispering Egrets**: Birds that dwell in the Hushed Marshes, known for their eerie calls. Locals believe they carry the voices of those who have passed on, warning of debts owed.

## Cultural Details

### Customs
- **Debt Anniversaries**: Families commemorate the day they borrowed time, marking it with a feast and sharing stories of lives changed by the decision to borrow. This day is both celebration and mourning.

### Taboos
- It is considered deeply disrespectful to speak ill of the deceased, especially in cases where debts remain unpaid. Such talk can invite bad luck and more debt.

### Festivals
- **The Tide of Time**: An annual festival celebrating the river Seru, where families gather to honor those lost to debt and the lives lived on borrowed time. Lanterns are set afloat, symbolizing hopes for the future.

## Internal Consistency Rules

1. **Time as Currency**: Time acts as a currency that directly impacts the lives of individuals. Borrowing it alters one's relationship with reality, creating a tangible cost to life's experiences.
  
2. **No Undoing Contracts**: Contracts with the Ledger Houses are unbreakable and must be honored, regardless of circumstances. Attempting to avoid repayment can lead to severe consequences, including public shaming and further debt.

3. **The Impact of Time Borrowing**: Those who borrow time experience a shift in their perception, as borrowed years feel slippery and elusive. The emotional weight of borrowed time can lead to psychological distress, especially when faced with the repercussions of inherited debt.
